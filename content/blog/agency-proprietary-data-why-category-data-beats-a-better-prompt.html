Agency proprietary data is the category knowledge an agency collects, structures and can query on demand: competitor creative, audience tensions, pitch outcomes and campaign results. It beats a better prompt because every agency can use the same models and copy the same prompts within a week. The data a model reads cannot be copied that easily, and it grows more valuable with every client in the category.

Key takeaways

  • Prompts are easy to share and models are available to everyone, so neither is a lasting advantage.
  • Category data compounds: each pitch, brief and campaign in a category makes the next one faster and sharper.
  • Published agency results show the gain comes from always-on category knowledge, not from one-off research sprints.
  • Independents cannot match holding-company identity data, but they can out-know them in the categories they choose.

Why isn't a better prompt a competitive advantage?

Because a prompt is a few hundred words anyone can read, share or rebuild. Agency conversations about AI tend to start with which model to use; the more useful question is what that model will read when it works on a client's category.

Clients are starting to ask the same thing. In June 2026 Digiday reported a 3C Ventures analysis finding that agency AI platform descriptions use nearly identical language, while "the underlying capabilities, however, vary dramatically" (Digiday). Its first recommended question for advertisers is what is proprietary.

A clever prompt does not answer that question. A record of every competitor ad in the category for the last two years, decoded and searchable, does.

Can independent agencies compete with holding-company data?

Not on identity data, but yes on category knowledge. The two are different kinds of moat.

The large groups have built their AI platforms on top of very large data assets. The Media Leader's overview notes that Publicis's Marcel uses Epsilon identity data, and that Omnicom's Omni counts 2.6 billion verified IDs, including Acxiom from the former IPG (The Media Leader). No independent is going to match that.

Category knowledge is different. Competitor ads, posts and audience conversation are public; what is scarce is the discipline to collect them continuously and decode them. An independent that tracks three categories in depth can walk into a pitch knowing more about that category than a network team that started last week.

Read from the agency's side of the table, that is a fair description of a data moat: knowing what others in the room do not.

How do you build a category data moat without a data science team?

Pick your categories, monitor them continuously and record your own outcomes against the same structure. Tooling now does the collection; your job is the discipline.

  1. Choose two or three categories where you have clients or want them. Depth beats breadth.
  2. Monitor always, not per pitch. Continuous tracking is what lets NEO360 and Havas Media start from a baseline.
  3. Use one structure. Tag everything by persona, proposition, tension and tone so it can be compared across brands and over time.
  4. Record your outcomes. Log pitch wins and losses, test results and the reasons, against the same tags.
  5. Make it queryable. Connect the archive to your team's AI assistant so a planner can ask questions of it in plain English.
  6. Separate client data. Keep each client's first-party data in its own space, and agree in the contract what moves with them if they leave.

How do you prove the moat to a client?

Show the evidence, not the tool. A claim about the category is only as strong as the posts and results behind it.

Put the trail in the deck: the competitor ads behind each "crowded" label, the sample size behind each tension, the past result behind each recommendation. FAQ

What is agency proprietary data?

It is the structured knowledge an agency owns and can query, such as decoded competitor creative, audience tensions, pitch records and performance history. It excludes client first-party data, which belongs to the client.

Do small agencies need their own data?

Yes, but not their own data infrastructure. Small agencies can use category-monitoring tools for collection and focus their effort on structure, enrichment and recording outcomes.

Is public social data really proprietary?

The raw posts are public. The continuous archive, the decoding by persona and tension, and your record of what worked against it are not, and that combination is what competitors cannot copy quickly.

Who owns the data when an agency uses client first-party data?

The client does. Agencies should access it under contract, keep it separate from their own category data and agree in advance how it is returned if the relationship ends.

How long does it take to build a category data advantage?

The first useful baseline can come from a single category audit, since tools can pull historical posts. The advantage grows with every month of monitoring and every recorded outcome.

Choose the category where you pitch most often and set up continuous monitoring this month. Before your next pitch in it, check what you know that the prospect and the other agencies do not; if the answer is thin, that is the gap to close first.
