The social listening tools that suit an agency are the ones that let you track several client brands in separate workspaces, report under your own name, cover your clients' languages, and charge in a way that does not punish growth. Judge them on five criteria, not on a ranking. As of 31 October 2026, most vendors tell agencies to ask for a quote.

Key takeaways

  • Choose on five agency criteria: multi-client workspaces, white-label reporting, language and source coverage, pricing model, and API access.

What should an agency look for in a social listening tool?

Look for five things: separate client workspaces, white-label reporting, language and source coverage, a pricing model that scales with clients, and an API. A tool that fails one of these will cost you hours on every client report.

What do the main vendors say about agencies?

Each vendor describes agencies differently, and some say nothing specific. These points come from the vendors' own pages, read on 31 October 2026. Check current plans before you decide, because they change.

  • Meltwater: its pricing page lists an Agency plan "designed for managing multiple clients", with tiers for small, growing and large agencies. It publishes no prices and says contracts are typically annual with a 12-month minimum (Meltwater pricing).
  • Talkwalker (now Lumen by Talkwalker): its pricing page offers three tiers, Core, Analyze and Business, by custom quote. Core includes unlimited users, and Business adds more workspaces with enterprise governance features. The page does not describe agency-specific features, though the site links to an agencies page (Talkwalker pricing).
  • Brand24: its pricing page lists published monthly prices, calls its Pro plan ideal for "small agencies" and its Business plan ideal for "established agencies", and lists an API as an extra fee on those plans (Brand24 prices).
  • Mention: its pricing page lists white-label reporting and API access as add-ons, and says the plan monitors social platforms, web sources and 75+ review sites (Mention pricing).
  • Sprout Social: its pricing page shows per-seat plans, and social listening as an add-on for the Standard plan and above, with no price listed (Sprout Social pricing).
  • Brandwatch: its home page describes a suite for consumer intelligence, social media management and influencer marketing, names itself as a Cision company, and links to agency pages (Brandwatch).

None of these pages gave a full list of monitored languages, so ask each vendor for it in writing for your clients' markets.

How does pricing differ for agencies?

Agency cost depends on the unit the vendor charges for. A per-seat model grows with your team, a mentions model grows with your clients' volume, and a custom quote depends on the scope you negotiate.

  • Per seat: good for small teams; costly if many account managers need access.
  • Per mention or keyword: predictable for small brands; risky when a client has a spike.
  • Custom quote: flexible, but hard to compare. Meltwater says its quotes depend on goals and modules, regions, data volume, users, integrations and contract terms.

How do you test a tool before you commit?

Use a two-week trial on real work.

  1. Pick two client brands in different languages.
  2. Build the same queries in each tool, including competitors.
  3. Check ten mentions per query by hand for relevance and language.
  4. Produce one client-ready report and time it.
  5. Ask what happens to cost if you add five clients next year.

Where does listening stop, and what comes next?

Listening shows what people say. It does not decode what competitors say in their ads, or which message nobody is using. FAQ

Which social listening tool is best for agencies?

There is no single best tool. The best one for you covers your clients' languages, separates client data, reports under your brand and prices in a way that fits your client mix. Test two or three on real briefs.

Do agencies need white-label reports?

Usually yes, because clients buy the agency's work. Some vendors include white-label reporting and some sell it as an add-on, so ask which applies to your plan.

Are agency plans cheaper?

Not necessarily. Meltwater lists an Agency plan but publishes no price, and several vendors give agencies no separate plan. Compare quotes line by line.

Can one workspace serve several clients?

It can, but separate workspaces are safer for data and access. Check whether each extra workspace costs more.
