Middle East travel marketing works best when each Gulf market gets its own persona mix rather than one regional message. SOMIN's study of 16,235 pieces of travel media from eight brands across six markets found adventure dominating everywhere, while luxury travel was under-served in Saudi Arabia, family travel was less crowded in Kuwait and thrifty travellers were an open lane in the UAE.

Key takeaways

  • Saudi Arabia is the region's largest travel economy: WTTC puts its sector at $178 billion, about 46% of the Middle East total in 2025.
  • Across Bahrain, KSA, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar and the UAE, the eight tracked travel brands defaulted to adventure, with Adventure Explorers the largest persona at 1,125 unique media.
  • Luxury Adventurers ranked 5th in KSA and Bahrain against 3rd to 4th in most markets, a gap worth testing.
  • Kuwait has specialists in luxury and thrift, which leaves family travel less contested there.
  • With WTTC forecasting a 2026 dip for the region, market-level testing protects budgets better than one regional campaign.

Why does Middle East travel marketing need to be personalised by market?

Because the six Gulf markets differ in size, traveller mix and what competitors already say, and a single regional message averages those differences away.

The scale gap alone is large. WTTC's economic impact research, published in April 2026, estimates the Middle East's Travel & Tourism sector grew 5.3% in 2025 against a 4.1% global average, contributing $385.8 billion to GDP. Saudi Arabia alone accounted for $178 billion, growing 7.4%, while the UAE contributed $68.5 billion with $56.9 billion in international visitor spending.

The traveller mix differs too. Saudi Arabia counted about 123 million tourists in 2025, of whom 93.3 million were domestic and 29.3 million international, according to the Ministry of Tourism's annual statistics as reported by Arab News. Roughly three in four Saudi tourists were travelling within the Kingdom, which is a different brief from selling an overseas holiday to families in Kuwait.

Demand is also volatile. In August 2026, WTTC forecast that the Middle East would be the only region to see Travel & Tourism GDP fall in 2026, by 14.5%, as conflict disrupts airspace and travel flows, while still expecting it to be the fastest-growing region over the next decade. A fixed regional plan struggles when conditions shift market by market.

What did SOMIN find across six Gulf markets?

Most travel brands talk to the same adventurous traveller, and the gaps sit in the secondary personas, which differ by market.

For the Wego case study, SOMIN read 16,235 pieces of paid and organic media from eight brands (Wego, ITL Group, Dadabhai, Alghanim Travel, KAPICO, Regency, Cozmo and Almulla) covering 2023 and 2024. It clustered the content into six personas, six topics, six relatability themes and six challenges, then split every cluster by market and brand.

  • Volume by market: Oman 3,267, UAE 3,196, Kuwait 2,968, Qatar 2,615, KSA 2,119 and Bahrain 2,070. Some pages are regional, so the total can double count.
  • Personas: Adventure Explorers led with 1,125 unique media, followed by Family Explorers in every market except Kuwait. Luxury Adventurers had 242 unique media, alongside the Soulful, Thrifty and Festive Explorers.
  • Topics: global travel destinations (491 unique media) and travel offers and packages (456) led.
  • Challenges brands tried to solve: cultural appreciation, recognition and celebration (178) and enhancing festive experiences and community connections (133).

Read as a whole, the region looks uniform. Read market by market, it does not.

Personalise the moment as well as the persona

A persona tells you who; a moment tells you when and why. SOMIN's moments-of-truth model, run on its audience-research workspace, surfaces travel moments such as planning a group trip, with the thought "I need to find a way to satisfy everyone's preferences while also enjoying the trip myself." It sits under a tension theme about social dilemmas and cultural expectations, which grouped 19 posts in a 220-post study in September 2026.

These moments are model-generated, not survey answers, but they map neatly onto Family and Festive Explorers. For those travellers, the message is less about the destination and more about keeping everyone happy, which fits the study's finding that brands already reach for festive experiences and community connection.

Why does one regional campaign underperform?

Because it tends to drift towards the market with the most volume or the fastest response, and the other markets end up hearing a message written for someone else.

The problem is common enough to show up in SOMIN's own data. In its ads-optimisation workspace, a 30-day study of 156 posts in September 2026 grouped 39 under a tension about engaging diverse markets, where brands struggle with a one-size-fits-all approach that produces weak cross-market branding. The same theme notes that outdated assumptions about audiences make it harder to engage them authentically and stand apart from competitors.

The practical answer is a shared brand platform with market-level personas, offers and creative underneath it. That keeps the brand consistent while letting KSA, Kuwait and the UAE each hear the message most likely to move them.

What should travel marketers measure in each market?

Measure your share of each persona against competitors, the response of each market's test cells, and how both move from month to month.

  • Persona share by market: how much of your content targets each persona compared with tracked competitors.
  • Test cells per market: each market's open-lane test against its own adventure control, never a regional average.
  • Cost per booking by persona: so a cheap click from the wrong persona does not look like a win.
  • Refresh rhythm: re-read competitor content quarterly, and sooner after a demand shock.

FAQ

Which Middle East market is the largest for travel and tourism?

Saudi Arabia. WTTC estimates its Travel & Tourism sector contributed $178 billion to GDP in 2025, about 46% of the Middle East total.

Should I run one GCC campaign or one per market?

Run one brand platform with market-level personas, offers and creative. SOMIN's Wego study found the open lanes differ by market: luxury in KSA, families in Kuwait and thrifty travellers in the UAE.

Which traveller personas do Gulf travel brands target?

SOMIN's study found six: Adventure Explorers, Luxury Adventurers, and the Soulful, Thrifty, Family and Festive Explorers. Adventure Explorers was the largest, with 1,125 unique media.

How do I find the gaps competitors leave open in each market?

Collect competitors' paid and organic content per market, cluster it into personas and topics, and compare each brand's share by market. A persona that ranks lower in one market than elsewhere is a candidate lane to test.

Pick the two markets that matter most to your next quarter, pull competitor content for each, and write one test brief per open lane.
