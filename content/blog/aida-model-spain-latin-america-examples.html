El modelo AIDA is a marketing framework whose letters stand for atención (attention), interés (interest), deseo (desire) and acción (action). It describes the four steps a message should guide a customer through, from noticing it to taking a step such as buying. In Spanish, "qué significa AIDA" in marketing always means these four stages.

Key takeaways

  • AIDA means atención, interés, deseo, acción: notice, get curious, want, act.
  • The model is usually credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis, from the late 1890s.
  • The structure is the same in Spain and Latin America, but the wording, channels and proof should be local.
  • "Latin America" is not one market: vocabulary, payment habits and trust signals differ by country.
  • Write for one country at a time, and have a local speaker review the copy.

¿Qué es el modelo AIDA?

It is a checklist for persuasive copy. Each letter is one job. In Spanish marketing you will also see it called "método AIDA", "fórmula AIDA" or "modelo AIDA", all meaning the same thing.

¿Quién creó el modelo AIDA?

It is usually credited to E. St. Elmo Lewis. According to Wikipedia's article on Lewis, Edward K. Strong Jr.'s 1925 book The Psychology of Selling and Advertising says that Lewis formulated "attract attention, maintain interest, create desire" in 1898, and added "get action" later. Lewis's own wording varied between his published versions.

¿Qué significa AIDA en publicidad?

In advertising, AIDA is a way to review a piece before it runs. Ask four questions of any ad: does the first line stop the person (atención), does the next line give a reason to continue (interés), is there proof and a benefit (deseo), and is there one clear step (acción)? What are AIDA examples for Spain and Latin America?

All three examples are hypothetical. They show how the same four steps change with the market.

Ejemplo 1: a Madrid online shoe shop (hypothetical)

  • Atención: an Instagram story that opens on a pair of trainers with the line "Cómodas desde el primer paso".
  • Interés: three slides on materials, sizing and how many sizes the shop stocks.
  • Deseo: customer photos and a plain statement of the returns policy.
  • Acción: "Compra ahora, devolución gratuita", with the delivery time shown.

Ejemplo 2: a Mexican fintech app (hypothetical)

  • Atención: a short video: "¿Cuánto pagas de comisiones sin darte cuenta?"
  • Interés: a 30-second explanation of how the account works and what it costs.
  • Deseo: a clear list of fees, a regulatory statement where required and a customer story.
  • Acción: "Abre tu cuenta en minutos", linking to a short sign-up.

In financial services, trust signals carry most of the desire stage. Check the rules that apply in your country before you claim anything about fees, safety or returns.

Ejemplo 3: a Colombian coffee subscription (hypothetical)

  • Atención: a photo of the farm and a headline naming the region.
  • Interés: a few lines on the harvest, the roast date and the grind options.
  • Deseo: a tasting note and a first-box offer.
  • Acción: a button to start the subscription, or a message to ask a question first.

The closing step can be a chat message as well as a button. Some brands sell through messaging apps, so offer the route your own customers use.

¿Cómo adaptar el modelo AIDA a cada mercado?

Keep the four stages and change what fills them. Five things usually need local work.

  • Vocabulary. Spanish differs by country. Everyday words for products, money and slang vary, so a phrase that sounds natural in Madrid may sound odd in Mexico City or Bogotá. Choose one market per version.
  • Form of address. Decide on tú, usted or vos by country and brand tone.
  • Channel. The first-contact channel and the final action channel may differ by market. Look at where your category's brands are active.
  • Proof. Local reviews, local press, local payment options and clear delivery terms build desire better than imported claims.
  • Moments. Holidays and seasons differ. Northern and southern hemisphere seasons are reversed, so a "summer sale" in July does not fit Santiago.

The same lesson holds in Asia. Our post on why localisation is not translation and the one on market-specific messaging make the case. SOMIN (SoMin.ai), the AI marketing-intelligence company run by Somin Pte. Ltd. in Singapore, lists Spain among its published markets.

¿Cómo ver qué hacen tus competidores en cada etapa?

Collect competitor ads for your country and label each one by AIDA stage and by the hook, proof and call to action it uses. If every rival opens with the same hook, that hook is crowded. FAQ

¿Qué significa AIDA en marketing?

AIDA significa atención, interés, deseo y acción. Es un modelo que describe los pasos que sigue un mensaje para convertir a una persona en cliente. In English: attention, interest, desire, action.

¿Es lo mismo "método AIDA" que "modelo AIDA"?

Yes. The names are used interchangeably, along with "fórmula AIDA". All refer to the same four-stage structure.

Does the AIDA model work the same in Spain and Latin America?

The four stages do, but the copy, proof, channel and offer should be local. Treat each country as its own market and avoid one neutral version for all.

Should I write in neutral Spanish?

"Neutral" Spanish can work for a first draft, but it often sounds foreign everywhere. For campaigns, write for one country and ask a local speaker to review it.

Are the examples in this article real campaigns?

No. All three are hypothetical and are written to show how the structure adapts to different markets.
