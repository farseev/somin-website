Enterprise social listening is usually sold by custom quote, so there is no standard price. Public price lists exist mostly for smaller plans: at the time of writing, Brand24 lists plans from $199 to $599 a month billed annually, with Enterprise from $1,499. Best value means the lowest cost per useful insight, not the lowest fee.

Key takeaways

  • Enterprise vendors such as Meltwater and Talkwalker say pricing is by custom quote and publish no figures.
  • Vendors charge by seat, by mentions or data volume, by queries or topics, or by a mix; the unit decides how cost grows.
  • A few vendors publish prices, but the cheap plans differ in mentions, keywords and features, so list prices are not like for like.
  • Measure value as cost per decision supported, including set-up, seats, overage and the analyst time needed.
  • No source we opened ranks vendors by value for money, so be wary of any list that does.

How is enterprise social listening priced?

Most enterprise tools price by custom quote, built from scope. Meltwater's pricing page says it "uses tailored pricing rather than a one-size-fits-all list", based on goals and modules, regions and sources, data volume, users, integrations and contract terms. It adds that contracts are typically annual with a 12-month minimum (Meltwater pricing).

Talkwalker's pricing page takes a similar line: choose a plan, then get a custom quote. It shows no dollar amounts. Its three tiers differ by volume of non-sampled results, topics, filters and channels, historical data, AI questions and workspaces (Talkwalker pricing).

What pricing models should you know?

There are four common units. Know which one you are buying.

ModelYou pay forWatch out for
Per seatEach named userCost rises as more colleagues need access
Mentions or data volumeHow much conversation is collectedSpikes, overage fees, sampling
Queries, keywords or topicsHow many things you trackLimits that force you to merge searches
Custom bundleModules, regions, support, contract termHard to compare, and add-ons creep in

Real vendors mix these. Sprout Social's pricing page shows per-seat plans, with social listening sold as an add-on from its Standard plan upward and no price listed (Sprout Social pricing). Talkwalker's Core tier includes unlimited users, so seat count matters less there; volume and topics matter more.

Which tools give the best value?

We cannot name a winner, and we found no independent source that ranks listening vendors on value. Value depends on your use case, so score vendors against it.

  • Small team, few brands: a published-price plan may be enough, if its mention and keyword limits fit.
  • Large volume, many markets: a quote-based tool may be cheaper per mention, but only if the languages and sources you need are in scope.
  • Agency with many clients: look at workspaces and reporting; see which social listening tools suit agencies.
  • Need to act on the data: count the analyst time that turns listening into a brief.

How do you compare value properly?

Work out the full cost for the same job, then divide by decisions supported.

  1. Write the scope: brands, competitors, languages, sources, history needed and users.
  2. Request quotes against that scope. Ask what is included and what is an add-on.
  3. Add hidden costs: onboarding (Meltwater says complex set-ups may carry a one-time charge), extra seats, overage, API and training.
  4. Test accuracy: check a sample of mentions for relevance and language.
  5. Count outputs: reports, briefs or decisions the tool helped produce in a month.

Raw volume is not the same as insight. What hidden costs should you plan for?

The quote is rarely the whole bill. Plan for these before you sign.

  • Onboarding and set-up: Meltwater's page says enterprise or complex set-ups may carry a one-time onboarding or implementation charge.
  • Overage: Brand24's page says extra mentions can incur overage fees.
  • Add-ons: API access, white-label reports and historical data are sold separately by some vendors.
  • People: someone has to write queries, clean results and turn them into decisions. That time is a real cost.

FAQ

How much does enterprise social listening cost?

There is no standard price. Enterprise vendors such as Meltwater and Talkwalker quote by custom scope and publish no figures on their pricing pages, so request quotes for the same scope.

Why do vendors hide their prices?

Vendors do not say. Their pages say the price depends on scope, such as modules, regions, data volume, users and contract term, which is why they ask you to contact sales.

Is a cheaper tool good value?

Only if its limits fit your work. A low fee with tight mention or keyword limits can cost more once you add overage or workarounds.

Which brands are best value for money?

We found no independent source that ranks them. Score vendors against your scope, trial two or three and compare the full cost.

What contract terms should I expect?

Meltwater's page says contracts are typically annual with a 12-month minimum. Brand24 and Sprout Social advertise annual billing options. Confirm terms in writing.
