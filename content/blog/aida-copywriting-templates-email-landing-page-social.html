An AIDA copy template gives each stage one slot: a hook for Attention, a relevant detail for Interest, proof and a benefit for Desire, and one clear step for Action. Fill the slots in order, then cut anything that does not serve its slot. The templates below cover email, landing pages and social posts, followed by a worked example for a meal-subscription launch.

Key takeaways

  • Write the four slots first, then polish the wording: structure beats style.
  • Email puts Attention in the subject line, landing pages put it in the headline, and social posts put it in the first line or frame.
  • Desire needs proof, such as a review, a figure you can source or a clear guarantee.
  • Each piece gets one call to action, and it states the cost or the commitment.
  • For a meal-subscription launch, a first-order discount by targeted email builds interest and desire; word of mouth alone or a single ad does not.

What is the AIDA copywriting formula?

It is a four-part order for a message: get noticed, hold interest, build want, ask for one step. Each part is short. If you want the background first, read our beginner's guide to AIDA.

What is an AIDA email template?

An email has two hooks, the subject line and the first sentence. Fill the template like this.

  • Subject line (Attention): [Specific result or curiosity] for [who]. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
  • First line (Attention): Name the reader's situation in one sentence.
  • Body (Interest): Two or three lines on how [product] handles [the problem]. One detail they did not know.
  • Proof (Desire): One quote, one figure you can source, or one photo. Then one sentence on how life looks after.
  • Button (Action): [Verb] + [object] + [cost or risk]. For example, "Claim your first box — cancel anytime".

A short line under the button, such as a deadline or a guarantee, removes last-minute doubt. Only state a deadline that is real.

What is an AIDA landing page template?

A landing page spreads the four stages down the page. The top of the page must do Attention and Interest at once, because visitors need to see at once that the page matches the ad they clicked.

  1. Headline (Attention): Repeat the promise of the ad that sent them here.
  2. Sub-headline and three bullets (Interest): What it is, who it is for, how it works.
  3. Proof block (Desire): Reviews, a photo or demo, and a plain comparison with the alternative.
  4. Objection strip (Desire): Price, delivery, cancellation. Answer the question people hesitate over.
  5. Button (Action): One primary button, repeated after the proof. Remove menus that lead away.

What is an AIDA social post template?

A social post compresses everything into a first line or first frame, a short middle and one prompt. Write the first line last, after you know what the post offers.

  • First line or frame (Attention): A question, number or image that fits the feed. No warm-up.
  • Middle (Interest): Two lines or a short carousel with one useful fact per slide.
  • Close (Desire): A result, a customer line or a behind-the-scenes shot that makes the product feel real.
  • Prompt (Action): "Tap to see the menu", "Reply with your postcode" or "Save for later". Pick one.

Different platforms reward different formats, so check what already performs in your category. Our post on what drives organic reach in 2026 is a starting point.

What does a worked AIDA example look like for a meal-subscription launch?

This is a hypothetical brand, "FreshCrate", launching a weekly meal-subscription box. The email below shows each slot filled.

  • Subject (Attention): "Dinner sorted for Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday".
  • First line (Attention): "If 6 pm still starts with 'what are we eating?', this is for you."
  • Body (Interest): "FreshCrate sends pre-portioned ingredients and a recipe card for three dinners a week. Each takes 30 minutes or less. You choose from six menus and can skip any week."
  • Proof (Desire): A photo of a finished dish, plus a customer line from the pilot group (in a real campaign, use a real, permitted quote).
  • Offer and button (Action): "Take 20% off your first box — skip or cancel anytime." Button: "Build my first box".

The percentage is an illustration only. Set any real discount from your own margins.

Which strategy builds interest and desire after a meal-subscription launch?

A common exam-style question asks: you have just launched a new meal-subscription service, so which marketing strategy builds interest and desire? The options are targeted email promotions with a discount for first-time customers, relying only on word of mouth, or running a single ad. The first is the answer.

  • Targeted email with a first-time discount. It reaches people already showing interest, repeats the message and gives a reason to try now. The discount lowers the risk of trying something new, which feeds desire. A button turns that desire into action.
  • Word of mouth alone. It is valuable but slow, and a new service has no customers yet to talk about it. You cannot control the message.
  • A single ad. It can create Attention, but one exposure rarely builds Interest or Desire. There is nothing to hold the person after the glance.

So the first option builds interest and desire because it is targeted, repeated and attached to an offer. Word of mouth and a one-off ad are weaker at those two stages.

How do you test and improve AIDA copy?

Change one slot at a time. Test two subject lines, then two proof blocks, then two buttons. If you change everything, you will not know which change worked. Compare against what rivals already say: if every competitor opens with the same hook, yours will not stand out. See why ads in one category look the same. FAQ

How long should AIDA copy be?

As long as it takes to do each job and no longer. A social post can do it in four lines. A landing page needs a full screen or more for proof and objections.

Which AIDA stage matters most?

The weakest one. Look at your numbers: low opens or thumb-stops mean Attention, short visits mean Interest, many visits but few sign-ups mean Desire or Action.

Can I use AI to write AIDA copy?

Yes, as a first draft, but check every claim and proof point by hand, because AI can invent facts. Our QA checklist for AI marketing content covers what to check.

What is the best call to action?

One verb, one object and the cost or risk stated, such as "Start free trial — no card needed". Offer a single step, not several.

Which strategy builds interest and desire for a new meal subscription?

Targeted email promotions with a discount for first-time customers. They reach interested people, repeat the message and give a reason to try now, which a single ad or word of mouth alone does not.
