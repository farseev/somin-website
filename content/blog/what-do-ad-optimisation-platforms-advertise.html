In their own ads, ad optimisation platforms mostly promise control: stop wasting budget, hand the daily optimisation busywork to AI, and make creative faster. SOMIN analysed 633 ads that Smartly, Madgicx, Strike Social and Adzooma ran on Meta and Google between 18 October 2025 and 17 October 2026. Of the 302 Meta ads, 72% pitched a product offer and 71% addressed a need for security, and the most common incentive across all 633 was a free trial.

Key takeaways

  • Two vendors placed 94% of the ads: Smartly ran 325 and Madgicx 271. SOMIN, Connectio and AdEspresso had no ads captured in the window.
  • The promise is relief and control. Empowerment (78%) and security (71%) were the most common needs in the Meta ads, and relief was the second most common emotion after confidence.
  • Offers split by sales model: 162 ads offered a free trial or free plan (158 of them Madgicx's), while Smartly's offers were reports, guides, demos and an event.
  • Proof is thin. Only 8 of 633 ads were testimonials or reviews, and the 44 ads that quoted a specific performance number gave little detail on how it was measured.
  • 630 of the 633 ads were in English. Some vendors reach Asia-Pacific markets, but APAC buyers see the category's global message, not a local one.

How was this study done?

We used SOMIN's ads-optimisation workspace, which tracks the paid and organic output of seven brands: SOMIN, Connectio, Smartly, Adzooma, Madgicx, Strike Social and AdEspresso. The paid ads come from the public Meta Ad Library and Google's Ads Transparency Center, captured by SOMIN's tracker.

  • Window: ads that were running at any point between 18 October 2025 and 17 October 2026. That is 633 ads: 302 on Meta and 331 on Google.
  • Labels: SOMIN's models tagged every ad for the customer need it addresses, its content type, tone, offer, format and placement. One ad can carry several labels, so shares add up to more than 100%.
  • A caveat on Google: 248 of the 331 Google ads were search text ads stored as rendered images with too little extractable text to classify. The message analysis below therefore uses the 302 Meta ads, which were all classified; volume and placement use all 633.
  • What counts do not show: Meta's Ad Library shows no spend or results for commercial ads, and neither does Google's centre, so a count measures how many ads a brand ran, not how much it spent.

The emotions tell the same story. Across all 633 ads, SOMIN's models tagged confidence in 330 and relief in 189, ahead of excitement (185) and hope (155). Frustration appeared in 57, usually as the problem the product removes.

The copy makes the fear concrete. Madgicx's ads talk about budgets on fire, 3am optimisation sessions and campaigns stuck in the learning phase, then offer an AI marketer that "does the prep, you just say yes". Smartly's ads promise to replace tool-hopping with one platform and quote outcomes such as up to 49% lower CPA and 33% more conversions on Meta.

The inferred audience is broad. Business owners appeared in 352 ads and entrepreneurs in 269, against 249 for marketing professionals, 48 for advertising agencies and 13 for media buyers. Much of the category is selling to the person who signs off the budget, not only to the specialist who runs it.

What offers and incentives do they use?

Free trials for the self-serve tools, content and events for the enterprise platform. The offer tells you how each vendor sells.

  • Free trials: 162 ads offered a free trial or free plan. 158 were Madgicx's, 58% of its 271 ads, and 120 named a 7-day free trial specifically. Adzooma offered a "forever free plan" instead.
  • Reports and guides: 42 ads offered a report, guide or benchmark download, 41 of them Smartly's, including its 2026 Digital Advertising Trends Report. Smartly also advertised its ADVANCE 2026 event.
  • Specific numbers: 44 ads put a figure in the offer, such as 3.3x faster ad creation, up to 49% lower CPA, 5.5x ROAS potential or 20 to 50% cost savings. Smartly ran 25 of them, Madgicx 10 and Strike Social 9.
  • Social proof: just 8 ads were testimonials or reviews, 4 each from Smartly and Madgicx.

Paid and organic content do different jobs. On their own channels the six competitors published 1,175 posts in the window, and 70% were thought leadership and 42% educational; only 2% carried a discount. In the Meta ads, thought leadership fell to 8% and product offers rose to 72%. The ads close; the organic posts persuade.

Which formats, tones and placements do they use?

Confident copy, image and dynamic-creative formats, and Meta's full set of placements for the trial-led vendor. Placement and format choices separate the vendors more clearly than tone does.

  • Tone: 97% of Meta ads were confident, 54% informative and 38% authoritative. Humour appeared in 49 ads, 47 of them Madgicx's, often sarcastic about manual media buying.
  • Format: about 120 Meta ads used dynamic creative with several versions, 108 a single image and 63 video. Smartly used dynamic creative in 85 of the 94 Meta ads we inspected; Madgicx leaned on single images (104) and video (44).
  • Call to action: Sign Up on 161 ads, all Madgicx's; Download on 42, all Smartly's; Learn More on most of the rest.
  • Meta placements: 156 of Madgicx's 187 Meta ads ran on all five surfaces at once: Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, Audience Network and Threads. Smartly kept to Facebook and Instagram, with about ten ads also on Audience Network and Threads. Strike Social's ads ran on Instagram only.
  • Google placements: 312 ads appeared on Google Search and its search partners, with 19 on YouTube and 18 each on Gmail, display partners and apps.
  • Run length: the median Madgicx Meta ad ran for about 11 days, the median Smartly ad 33 days.

Language is the gap for Asia. 630 of the 633 ads were in English, yet some of them clearly target the region. Google's transparency data lists where each ad was shown: 56 of Madgicx's 84 Google ads reached at least one APAC market, from India and Australia to Thailand and the Philippines, against 22 of Smartly's 230 and none of Adzooma's 17. Smartly also tags its landing links by region, and of the 54 tagged links in the Meta ads we inspected, 22 pointed to North American campaigns, 23 to EMEA, 6 to the UK and 3 to APAC.

What does this say about the ad optimisation category?

It shows two sales motions, a crowded set of messages and a race to claim prediction. Each matters to a buyer.

Two motions. Madgicx and Adzooma sell self-serve, with free trials or a free plan; Madgicx adds Sign Up buttons and short-lived Meta ads. Smartly sells to enterprise teams: longer-running ads, downloads, events and demos. Neither is better; they suit different buyers, and the ads make the difference visible before you speak to sales.

Conquest search. Vendors bid on each other. Adzooma ran a Google ad headlined "Your Madgicx Alternative", Smartly ran "Try Smartly Instead" variants, and a Madgicx search ad called itself the top AdEspresso alternative. When you search a vendor's name, expect a competitor's pitch next to it.

Repetition. Madgicx's 187 Meta ads carried 55 distinct primary texts, and the 94 Smartly Meta ads we inspected carried 17. Vendors test variations of a few messages, and those messages sound alike across brands. We look at why categories converge in why ads in one category all look the same.

Prediction is becoming table stakes. One Smartly ad says its platform "predicts performance before you launch". SOMIN makes a similar promise, so we explain how such predictions work, and where they fail, in what creative clickability is.

How should a buyer read ad optimisation claims?

Treat every number in an ad as a hypothesis to test on your own account. Ads have no room for methodology, so ask for it.

Claim typeExample in the adsWhat to ask
"Up to" performance gainsUp to 49% lower CPAWhat is the median result, across how many accounts, over what period, against what baseline?
Speed multipliers3.3x faster ad creationFaster than which workflow, measured how, for what volume of creative?
Return potential5.5x ROAS potentialWhose accounts, at what spend level, and is "potential" a typical or a best case?
Risk reversalUpgrade only after you see ROIHow is ROI attributed, and does the tool's own tracking decide it?
AutopilotRun Meta ads on autopilotWhich decisions does the tool take alone, and where do approvals sit?
PredictionPredicts performance before you launchCan we see predicted against actual results on our own data?

Three habits help. Run any trial on a defined slice of spend with a holdout, so the comparison is fair. Ask for two customer references in your market, since the ads offer little social proof. And for APAC teams, check support hours and local-language capability, because the advertising will not tell you.

FAQ

Which ad optimisation platform advertises the most?

In SOMIN's capture for 18 October 2025 to 17 October 2026, Smartly ran the most ads (325), followed by Madgicx (271). Smartly concentrated on Google search, while Madgicx ran most of its ads on Meta.

What is the most common offer in ad optimisation ads?

A free trial. 162 of the 633 ads offered a free trial or free plan, almost all of them Madgicx's, and 120 named a 7-day trial. Enterprise-focused ads offered reports, guides, demos and events instead.

Are claims like "up to 49% lower CPA" reliable?

They may be true for some customers, but an ad cannot show the base, sample or period behind them. Ask for the median result and the method, then test on a slice of your own spend with a holdout.

Do ad optimisation vendors localise their ads for Asia?

Rarely, on this evidence. 630 of 633 ads were in English, even though 56 of Madgicx's 84 Google ads were shown in at least one APAC market. Only 3 of 54 region-tagged Smartly landing links pointed to APAC campaigns.

How can I see what competitors advertise?

Search their Page in the Meta Ad Library and their name in Google's Ads Transparency Center, record each ad with consistent tags, and repeat the capture regularly, because commercial ads outside the EU and UK disappear from Meta's library once they stop running.
