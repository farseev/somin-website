To measure social media influence in 2026, look past follower count to what an audience does with each post: engagement per post, shares and saves, watch time and retention, the share of a topic a creator owns against similar accounts and, where you sell, attributed sales. Instagram now recommends content post by post on engagement, so influence is earned with every post rather than banked in a follower total.

Key takeaways

Why are follower counts no longer a reliable measure of influence?

Because platforms no longer distribute content mainly by follower count. Reach is decided post by post, so a follower total tells you the size of an audience a creator once earned, not how many people their next post will move.

Instagram's creator team set this out in February 2025: a reel is first shown to a small audience of non-followers who are likely to enjoy it, then to wider audiences if it performs, with signals that include watch time, retention, shares, likes and comments. In Instagram's own words, it distributes content "based on its engagement, not just the creator's follower count".

Which metrics matter for measuring influence in 2026?

Measure attention, intent and ownership first, then outcomes. Each metric below answers a different question, and no single number replaces the set.

What do real creator portfolios show about influence?

They show that scale, posting volume and influence are three different things, and that totals hide most of what matters.

Daisy Iggy: warmth beats scale

SOMIN read a year of the Singapore pet creator's content, from July 2025 to June 2026, against four dog accounts chosen by cosine similarity on shared audience interests, from 0.83 down to 0.61. Seventy per cent of her content is connection-led, against 8% of competitor content in that territory, and her lead theme earns 10.4k engagements per post.

Her cosy-connection posts earn about 10,400 engagements each against roughly 3,900 for her style posts, about 2.7 times as much. Caltex Malaysia: one creator carries the total

Caltex's four activated creators earned 6.0M engagements in 90 days, and 5.8M of them came from one creator. The other three earned 118K, 10K and 203. As a single roster number the programme looks healthy; read per creator, it is one dominant voice and three much smaller ones at different stages. The Caltex creators case study gives each one a distinct territory instead of ranking them on one scale.

Vamp: volume is not influence

Across 8 creators and 81 posts, Vamp's roster earned 2.8M engagements over 12 months. One photography creator earned 100K per post across 8 posts, the best rate on the roster, while the creator who posted most, 28 times, earned 27 per post. The Vamp portfolio case study shows how each creator got one lane nobody else on the roster holds.

All three are engagement-only reads. None includes sales figures, so engagement stands in as a proxy for reach, not revenue, and the case studies say so.

How do you connect influence to sales?

Give each creator a trackable path to purchase and read sales per post and per creator, not only per campaign.

Budgets are moving faster than measurement. The IAB projects US creator ad spend at $37 billion in 2025, up 26% year on year, with 40% of buyers ranking overall ROI as their top KPI for creator campaigns. The same report says brands want better attribution and more consistent reporting.

  • Use creator-specific codes, links or shop data so every sale has an owner.
  • Compare GMV per post with engagement per post, and look for creators whose attention converts.
  • Decode why top sellers sell: what they say, how they present the product and how they frame the hook.

How do you build an influence scorecard?

Pick a fair comparison set, report per post, weight shares, saves and retention above likes, and refresh on a schedule.

  1. Choose the comparison set. Match on audience, platform and size; similarity scores like those in the Daisy Iggy read keep it honest.
  2. Report per post and per creator. Add concentration so one outlier cannot hide a weak roster.
  3. Weight the deeper signals. Shares, saves and retention say more about influence than likes.
  4. Track topic share monthly. Influence shows up as owning a theme that rivals do not.
  5. Add sales where you can. Label engagement-only reads clearly so nobody mistakes reach for revenue.

FAQ

What is a good engagement rate on social media in 2026?

There is no universal figure; it varies by platform, format and audience size. Benchmark against a set of similar accounts and track the per-post trend rather than chasing an industry average.

Do follower counts still matter?

They still matter for reaching existing followers and as a quick filter. But platforms such as Instagram now push posts to non-followers based on early engagement, so recent per-post performance predicts reach better than follower count.

How do you measure influencer ROI?

Attribute sales with creator-specific codes, links or shop data, then compare GMV per post with what the creator costs. Where sales cannot be tracked, report engagement as a proxy for reach and say so.

What is topic share for a creator?

The share of a defined theme a creator's content holds compared with similar accounts. In SOMIN's Daisy Iggy read, connection-led content was 70% of her voice against 8% for competitors.

Pull the last 90 days for every creator you work with, recalculate engagement per post and concentration, and check whether the roster still looks the way follower totals suggest.
