Social listening and social media management vendors mostly post confident, informative thought leadership on LinkedIn, written for business owners and increasingly about AI. In SOMIN's analysis of 1,339 organic posts from 18 vendors between 20 July and 17 October 2026, 74% were classed as thought leadership and 55% went out on LinkedIn. The posts that earned the most engagement, though, were about people: company culture, events and hiring.

Key takeaways

  • Five vendors (Meltwater, Buffer, Agorapulse, Emplifi and Sprinklr) published 68% of the 1,339 posts. Talkwalker, Keyhole and BuzzSumo published none on the channels we track in that window.
  • LinkedIn carried 734 posts, Instagram 399 and Facebook 206. Over the full year, LinkedIn's share was 58% of 6,555 posts.
  • One caption in three (33%) mentions AI, from 63% of Hootsuite's posts to 7% of Zoho Social's.
  • Educational posts are the second most common type (45% of posts), yet they beat their own vendor's typical engagement only 35% of the time. Company culture posts did so 69% of the time.
  • Proof is scarce: case studies were 1% of posts, and only 7% were aimed at data analysts, the people who use these tools every day.

How did we analyse the vendors' content?

We used a SOMIN workspace that tracks SOMIN and 18 social listening and social media management vendors: Meltwater, Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Talkwalker, Statusbrew, Digimind, Brand24, Agorapulse, Synthesio, Awario, Buffer, Keyhole, Emplifi, Sendible, BuzzSumo, YouScan, Zoho Social and Sprinklr. SOMIN's own posts are left out so the study covers the 18 vendors only.

The main window is the 90 days to 18 October 2026: 1,339 organic posts published between 20 July and 17 October on the channels this workspace tracks, which are LinkedIn company pages, Instagram and Facebook. X, YouTube and TikTok are not included. As a cross-check we also counted the 12 months from 18 October 2025, which holds 6,555 posts.

SOMIN's models tag every post with topics, content types, tones and an inferred audience. A post can carry several labels, so shares add up to more than 100%. Engagement is likes or reactions plus comments plus shares, as SOMIN records them.

Raw engagement mostly reflects audience size, so a big brand's weakest post can outscore a small brand's best. To compare fairly we set each post against the median for its own vendor on the same platform, then report the share of posts in a group that beat that baseline. We call it the beat rate; across all posts it is 48%, so that is par. Instagram Stories carry no public engagement counts and Facebook posts had a median of one engagement, so engagement comparisons use the 969 LinkedIn and Instagram feed posts.

What topics do social listening tools post about?

Mostly work, technology and business, framed for professionals. SOMIN's topic model tagged 71% of posts as Career (posts about people's working lives, skills and growth), 67% as Technology, 48% as Business and 43% as Marketing. Smaller themes were events (15%), social media itself (14%), promotions (13%) and finance (10%).

AI shows up in two ways. Only 8% of posts are mainly about AI, but 33% of captions mention it, through words such as AI, generative AI, LLMs or agentic. The spread between vendors is wide:

  • Most AI-heavy: Hootsuite (63% of captions), Emplifi (51%), Synthesio (50%), Sprinklr (43%) and Meltwater (40%).
  • Least AI-heavy: YouScan (19%), Sprout Social (9%) and Zoho Social (7%).

On LinkedIn, few posts use hashtags at all (172 of 734). Of those that do, #ai was the most common, followed by #consumerinsights, #customerexperience and #sociallistening.

The inferred audience is broader than the buyer. SOMIN tagged 75% of posts as addressing entrepreneurs and 72% business owners, against 35% for marketing professionals, 18% for social media managers and 7% for data analysts. The people who spend their day inside a listening tool are rarely the ones being spoken to.

What gets the most engagement?

People, events and well-timed data stories. Four patterns stand out.

Company culture beats everything else that is common. Culture posts had a median of 33 engagements against 16 for thought leadership, and 69% beat their vendor's baseline. The 12-month data agrees: on LinkedIn and Instagram feed posts (5,188 in all), culture posts averaged 68.9 engagements against 42.9 overall. Buffer's hiring and four-day-week posts, Sprinklr's internship wrap-up and Emplifi's executive appointments all fit the pattern.

Warm tones beat authoritative ones. Posts tagged hopeful (78% beat rate), appreciative (78%) and enthusiastic (64%) did far better than authoritative (37%), educational (36%) or helpful (31%) posts.

Events work. Posts on the events topic beat their baseline 65% of the time, and all 14 event recaps on LinkedIn and Instagram did so, though that is a small sample.

Data meets culture. The two most engaged posts in the whole set were Digimind's Instagram analyses of social conversation around New York and Paris Fashion Weeks, with 4,635 and 1,903 engagements. Meltwater's brand campaign Reels with Kim Cattrall drew about 31,000 and 28,000 video views. Both pair the vendor's product with something the audience already cares about.

Mentioning AI, by contrast, made little difference: posts that mention it beat their baseline 50% of the time, against 47% for posts that do not.

What does this tell a marketer choosing a social listening tool?

A vendor's feed tells you how it wants to be seen, not how well its product works. Use it as one signal among several.

  • Read the positioning. AI-heavy feeds show where a vendor is putting its roadmap and its sales story. Feeds full of how-to posts and product walkthroughs suggest a self-serve, workflow-led product. A feed led by culture posts tells you about the company more than the tool.
  • Do not read engagement as quality. Engagement follows audience size, employee networks and posting habits. A vendor with a large, active team on LinkedIn will out-engage a more capable tool with a small one.
  • Test on your own data. Ask each shortlisted vendor to run your brand and two competitors through a trial, and judge the output on questions you already know the answer to.

What does it tell a vendor competing in this category?

The crowded space is confident, informative thought leadership for business owners. The open space is proof and people.

  • Show proof. Case studies were 1% of posts and testimonials 6%. A category that sells evidence publishes very little of its own.
  • Talk to the practitioner. Analysts and social media managers are addressed in a small minority of posts. Content built around their daily problems has little competition.
  • Put people forward. Culture, events and employee voices earn more than the brand speaking for itself. On LinkedIn, posts written by people and reshared by the company page earned about twice the median engagement of page-written posts; the detail is in our LinkedIn engagement study.
  • Use your own data topically. Only 4% of posts were topical. The best example in the set, the fashion-week analyses, used the vendor's own product to say something timely.

FAQ

Which social listening vendor posts the most on social media?

In the 90 days to 18 October 2026, Meltwater published the most organic posts on LinkedIn, Instagram and Facebook (250), followed by Buffer (215). Over the 12 months to the same date, Buffer led with 1,469 posts.

Which platform do social listening vendors use most?

LinkedIn. It carried 734 of the 1,339 posts in the 90-day window (55%) and 58% of the 6,555 posts over 12 months. Instagram and Facebook make up the rest of the channels we track.

How much social listening vendor content is about AI?

About a third. 33% of captions mention AI, generative AI, LLMs or agentic AI, although only 8% of posts are mainly about AI. Hootsuite, Emplifi, Synthesio, Sprinklr and Meltwater mention it most often.

What type of post earns social listening vendors the most engagement?

Company culture posts, such as hiring, team and milestone posts. They had a median of 33 engagements against 16 for thought leadership, and 69% beat their own vendor's typical engagement. Event posts and warm, appreciative tones also did well.

Does a vendor's social engagement say anything about product quality?

No. Engagement mostly reflects audience size, employee networks and posting habits. Use a vendor's feed to understand its positioning, then judge the product on a trial with your own brand and competitors.
