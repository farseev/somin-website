Mothercare Singapore's strategy teaches that new mothers respond to peers more than to broadcast. SOMIN (SoMin.ai), the AI marketing-intelligence company run by Somin Pte. Ltd. in Singapore, combined competitor ad data with GWI behavioural data to localise HQ messaging, with a fun, bold, relatable tone and content on parenting challenges and quality products.

Key takeaways

  • Global brand direction is not a local strategy; Mothercare Singapore's team tracked over 4,000 category ads a year by hand before adopting a data-led process.
  • Parenting-challenge content earned about 39 engagements per post, the highest yield of the three themes ranked.
  • Singapore mothers rely more on peer opinions, reviews and social recommendations than the average Singaporean.
  • The recommended tone is fun, bold, modern and slightly cheeky, with young, relatable creators rather than authority figures.
  • Reported results: 80% less time on audience research and idea development, 3x more refined segmentation, 74% cost savings and 25+ hours a week reclaimed.

What was Mothercare Singapore's challenge?

The team had clear direction from global HQ but no systematic read of the local market. Mothercare's Singapore business, in its 40th year, runs always-on paid social on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok and keeps every campaign on-brand with HQ messaging.

The case page lists four problems:

  • Over 4,000 category ads a year tracked by hand.
  • Always-on campaigns on three platforms at once.
  • Hours lost weekly to manual research that still ended in guesswork.
  • No way to tell which content would land with Singaporean parents.

This is the localisation problem in one line. Our piece on why global brands need market-specific messaging explains why it recurs.

What approach did SOMIN take?

Two steps: competitor ad intelligence, then GWI-powered personas. Demographics explain who buys; psychographics explain why. SOMIN built on psychographics and content preference.

  1. Competitor ad intelligence: how competitors win the audience on every ad platform in the category.
  2. GWI-powered personas: personas turned into content ideas using GWI data on local interests, habits and online behaviour.

SOMONITOR benchmarked Mothercare against Baby Kingdom, Babyland, Motherswork, Cotton On Kids, Mummy's Market, First Few Years, Hatchery Cribs, Kiddy Palace, Pramfox and Pupsik. For the wider method, see market personas from audience data and competitor ads.

Which content themes earned the most engagement?

Parenting-challenge content earned the most engagement per post. Of five recurring themes, two had too little volume to rank. The other three ranked by post-to-engagement ratio:

The two themes with less volume were safe, comfortable spaces for babies and baby-care concerns. The lesson is to lead with what parents struggle with, then show product value. The product theme has the most volume; the struggle theme gets more response per post.

How do Singapore mothers decide who to trust?

They trust peers and community validation more than the average Singaporean. According to the GWI data in the case study, peer opinions, reviews and expert endorsement outweigh brand announcements.

The channels furthest above the national baseline (index 100) are celebrity or influencer endorsements (147.3) and social media recommendations or comments (131.0). That is why paid social with relatable creators matters here. If you work with creators, see how to measure influence beyond follower counts.

What tone suits Singapore mothers?

Fun, bold, modern and stylish, with a slightly cheeky edge. The case study's tone brief asks for young, relatable creators, not authority figures, so mothers feel like themselves first and caregivers second.

The brand traits Singapore mothers over-index on, against a Singapore average of 100, include:

  • Young (134.6)
  • Funny (123.4)
  • Bold (119.8)
  • Trendy or cool (119.4)
  • Exclusive (118.0)

They under-index on authentic (91.1), smart (89.2) and reliable (86.9), so an expert, serious tone is a weaker fit than a playful one.

How was the audience segmented?

Into six GWI parenting personas. The largest is Parenting-Focused Workers at 53.9% (about 1.3 million people), followed by Parental Investors (22.6%), Thrifty Parents (20.6%), Traditional Parents (10.2%), Career-Focused Parents (7.1%) and Young Parents (4.6%).

For the largest segment, SOMIN built ready-to-run ads. One used a real objection, "most new mums get overwhelmed with baby gear cluttering their home", for an activity gym instead of a spec sheet. Another focused on mum logistics for a stroller: storage, a one-handed fold and single-to-double conversion.

What results did Mothercare report?

The case page reports four results:

  • 80% less time on audience research and idea development.
  • 3x more refined segmentation of expecting mothers' needs and personas.
  • 74% cost savings versus traditional campaign execution.
  • 25+ hours a week reclaimed from manual research and guesswork.

These are results for one brand. SOMIN's own case page notes that outcomes vary by brand and market.

What can other brands take from this?

  1. Localise with data, not just translation. HQ direction needs a local read. See content localisation in Asia is not translation.
  2. Lead with the parent's struggle. It earned the most engagement per post.
  3. Use peer voices. Influencer and social recommendations carry the most weight with this audience.
  4. Match tone to the audience, not the category habit. Fun and bold over expert and serious.
  5. Turn research into ads in the same step. The data that saved time also produced the creative.
