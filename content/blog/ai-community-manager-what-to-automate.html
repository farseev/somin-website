An AI community manager is an agent that reads comments and direct messages, sorts them, drafts replies and sends the right ones to people. Automate the repetitive, low-risk work: tagging, routing, answers to common questions and first replies. Keep complaints, crises, legal or medical questions, influencer and press contacts, and anything emotional with a person.

Key takeaways

  • Start with sorting and drafting, not with auto-posting.
  • Write the rules first: topics the agent may answer, topics it must pass on, and the tone it uses.
  • Give the agent approved answers to draw from, so it does not invent facts.
  • Measure response time and escalation quality, not just volume.

What can an AI community manager do?

It can do four jobs well. It sorts incoming comments and messages by topic, sentiment and urgency. It drafts replies from your approved answers. It routes messages to the right team, such as sales, support or PR. And it reports on what people are asking about, which is useful for content and product teams. A good middle ground is draft-and-approve: the agent writes the reply, a person clicks send. Move a topic to full automation only when its drafts have needed no edits for several weeks.

How do you set the rules before switching it on?

  1. List the topics. Export a month of comments and DMs and group them. Most brands find a handful of topics make up most of the volume.
  2. Write approved answers. One short answer per common question, with links. The agent should only state facts that are in these answers.
  3. Define escalation. Name the words, topics and sentiment that send a message to a person, and who that person is.
  4. Set the tone. Give examples of good replies in your brand voice, in each language you support.
  5. Decide the hours. If people only review drafts in office hours, say so in the first auto-reply.

What should you measure?

Track time to first reply, the share of messages resolved without a person, the share of drafts edited before sending, and escalations that came too late. Read a sample of automated replies every week. A rising edit rate means the answers or rules need updating.

What are the risks?

The main risks are wrong facts, a tone that feels robotic, and replying to the wrong thing, such as a joke or sarcasm. Platforms also have their own rules for automated messaging, so check each platform's policies. Tell people when they are talking to an automated assistant if your market or platform expects it.

FAQ

What is an AI community manager?

An AI agent that reads comments and messages on social media, sorts them, drafts replies and passes sensitive ones to people.

Will an AI community manager replace people?

It takes over repetitive replies and sorting. People still handle complaints, crises, partnerships and judgement calls, and they own the rules the agent follows.

Should replies be posted automatically?

Start with draft-and-approve. Automate a topic fully only after its drafts have been accurate for several weeks.

Can it reply in several languages?

Yes, but give it approved answers and tone examples in each language, and have a native speaker review samples.
