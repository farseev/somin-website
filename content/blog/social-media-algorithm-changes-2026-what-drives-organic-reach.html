Social media algorithm changes in 2026 have moved organic reach away from follower counts towards per-post performance and feeds that people steer themselves. Meta, Threads and TikTok now let users tell the algorithm which topics they want, and Meta cuts distribution for accounts that recycle other people's work. What still drives reach is original content that holds attention, earns shares and is clearly about one topic.

Key takeaways

  • Reach is earned per post: Instagram tests reels on small audiences of non-followers and widens distribution when they perform.
  • Users now steer feeds directly, through Facebook's Your Algorithm, Threads' Dear Algo and Your Algo, and TikTok's Manage Topics.
  • Recycled content costs reach: Meta reduces distribution for accounts that repeatedly reuse others' videos, photos or text.
  • Topic clarity, platform-native formats and people-led posting are the levers brands still control.
  • Track changes through official platform sources and your own per-post data, not rumours.

What changed in social media algorithms in 2025 and 2026?

Two shifts stand out: platforms distribute on early engagement from people who do not follow you, and they hand users explicit controls over the topics they see.

The common thread is that the follower graph matters less than it did. Meta says its ranking systems infer interests mainly from what people like, comment on and watch, alongside thousands of other signals, and users can now steer those inferences in plain language.

Why are brands seeing less organic reach?

Because each post now competes for recommendation slots that are open to every account, and audiences can switch whole topics off.

What still drives organic reach in 2026?

Originality, attention, shareability and topic clarity, delivered in each platform's native format.

Original work over recycled clips

Meta's July 2025 guidance says reusing approved material is fine if you add your own value through editing, voiceover or commentary, but stitching clips together or adding a watermark does not count. It also says Facebook captions with no links, minimal capitals and no more than five hashtags perform best.

Attention in the first seconds

Instagram names watch time and retention among its ranking signals, and a reel's first small test audience decides whether it travels further. Use trial reels to test openings before your followers see them.

One clear topic per post

When people can tell Facebook, Threads or TikTok which topics they want, a post that is unmistakably about one subject is easier to match. Mixed-message posts serve nobody's request.

People, not only logos

LinkedIn's Pages guide says companies that post weekly see twice the engagement, that video gets five times more engagement, and that Pages can notify employees of key posts up to once a week to extend organic reach. Brand reach increasingly travels through people.

How should brands approach Threads and LinkedIn in 2026?

Treat Threads as a community platform and LinkedIn as a people-first one; both reward consistent, specific voices over broadcast posts.

Threads

SOMIN's social-listening model also surfaces the thought "I want to learn how to use Threads for better SEO and engagement." Meta's June 2026 update credits Threads' growth to 500 million monthly users to communities, now out of beta with a hub and progress tracking, plus Your Algo, which lets people privately steer their feed for one, three or seven days. We found no official Meta statement on how Threads posts rank in web search, so treat Threads SEO as discoverability inside Threads: post into the relevant communities, keep each post on one topic, and reply where the conversation already runs.

LinkedIn

Sameness is part of the LinkedIn problem. When everyone drafts with the same tools, posts converge. Pair distinct voices with LinkedIn's basics: weekly posting, native video, a complete Page and three to five relevant hashtags.

How do you keep up with algorithm changes without chasing rumours?

Read official sources, test changes on your own account and judge by per-post data.

  1. Follow the primary sources. Meta's newsroom and creator blogs, TikTok's newsroom and LinkedIn's own guides announce the changes that matter.
  2. Listen to your category monthly. See which themes and formats are landing before you change your plan.
  3. Test one variable at a time. Openings, length, format and topic, each against a control.
  4. Measure per post. Retention, shares, saves and comments tell you more than follower growth.
  5. Pick platforms deliberately. SOMIN's insight "Overreaching in confidence often leads to hidden pitfalls" warns that brands overestimate how many platforms they can run well, drawing on 69 posts.

FAQ

Did organic reach drop in 2026?

There is no official cross-platform figure for organic reach. What changed is how reach is allocated: per post, by early engagement and increasingly by the topics users choose, which hurts accounts that relied on follower distribution.

Do hashtags still matter in 2026?

Less than topic clarity. Meta says Facebook captions with no more than five hashtags perform best, and LinkedIn suggests three to five relevant hashtags on Page posts.

Does AI-generated content get less reach?

Not automatically. But TikTok has announced a Manage Topics setting that lets users see less AI-generated content and requires creators to label realistic AI content, and Meta reduces distribution for unoriginal, recycled posts.

How often do social media algorithms change?

The major platforms announced new feed controls or policies several times between 2025 and 2026, as the table above shows, and Meta now says its ranking adapts in real time to user requests. Plan around announced changes and confirm everything else with your own per-post data.
