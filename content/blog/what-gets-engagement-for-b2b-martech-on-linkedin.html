On LinkedIn, B2B marketing-technology content gets the most engagement when a person writes it, it carries a single image, and it is about people, culture or events rather than a how-to. In SOMIN's analysis of 734 LinkedIn company-page posts from 12 social listening and social media management vendors, published between 20 July and 17 October 2026, posts written by people and reshared by the brand page earned a median of 31.5 engagements against 15 for posts the page wrote itself. Single-image posts beat video, documents and text.

Key takeaways

  • Almost a third of the posts (31%) were written by a person and reshared by the company page. They earned twice the median engagement and nearly four times the comments of page-written posts.
  • Single-image posts were 46% of the sample and the strongest format: a median of 25 engagements, against 15 for video and for documents and 11.5 for posts with no media.
  • Company culture posts beat their vendor's usual engagement 70% of the time. Educational posts, half of everything published, did so 37% of the time.
  • Appreciative, hopeful and enthusiastic posts beat authoritative and urgent ones by a wide margin.
  • One-line posts, questions to the audience and stacks of hashtags did not help. One or two hashtags did slightly better than none.

How did we measure LinkedIn engagement?

We used SOMIN's social listening workspace, which tracks 18 social listening and social media management vendors. Twelve of them published on a LinkedIn company page in the 90 days to 18 October 2026: Buffer (186 posts), Meltwater (116), Emplifi (98), Sprinklr (84), Agorapulse (56), Sprout Social (44), Synthesio (34), Brand24 (33), YouScan (33), Hootsuite (23), Sendible (16) and Zoho Social (11).

LinkedIn volume is not thin in this category. It carried 55% of the vendors' organic posts in the 90-day window and 3,797 posts over the 12 months to 18 October 2026, so we did not need to fall back on a platform comparison. We used the 734 posts from the 90-day window for the detailed analysis and the 12-month figures as a cross-check.

Engagement is reactions plus comments plus shares as SOMIN records them. LinkedIn reposts are tracked separately (3.5 per post on average) and are not included. Because a large page will always out-score a small one, we compare each post with the median for its own vendor on LinkedIn and report the beat rate: the share of posts in a group that beat their vendor's median. Across all 734 posts the beat rate is 48%, so anything well above that is doing better than par.

A single image. It was the most used format and the best performer on every measure we looked at.

Person-written posts lean towards images, so we checked page-written posts on their own. The order held: images had a median of 19 and a 56% beat rate, video 14 and 43%, documents 14 and 32%.

This differs from the headline in LinkedIn's own Pages best practices, which says video gets five times more engagement, without saying compared with what. The same guide says images typically bring a two times higher comment rate, and that matches what we saw: image posts averaged 6.1 comments, video 2.9. In this category, at least, video is not a shortcut to engagement. Use it when motion explains something an image cannot.

Do posts written by people beat posts from the brand page?

Yes, by a wide margin. Of the 734 posts, 230 were written by a person (an employee, an executive, a creator or a partner) and then reshared by the company page.

  • Median engagement: 31.5 for person-written posts, 15 for page-written posts.
  • Comments: 8.8 per post against 2.4.
  • Beat rate: 58% against 43%.

Buffer accounts for most of these posts: 159 of its 186 LinkedIn posts were reshared from its team. Taking Buffer out makes the effect stronger, not weaker. The other vendors' 71 person-written posts had a median of 37 and beat their vendor's baseline 79% of the time.

The biggest posts in the sample show why. Buffer engineers' hiring posts, with a fully remote, four-day-week pitch, reached 2,861 engagements for the top one. A creator's post about attending Dreamforce with Sprout Social drew 328 engagements, 111 of them comments. When a page reshares a member's post, the counts reflect that person's network as well as the page's, and that wider network is the point.

LinkedIn's guide makes the same suggestion: highlight the brand by resharing mentions from employees, executives and key customers.

By topic, posts about AI (65 posts, median 27, 62% beat rate) and events (75 posts, median 25, 65%) did well. Hiring posts were the strongest single theme, with a median of 128 engagements across 23 posts, but 20 of them came from Buffer, so treat that as one company's result.

Which tone of voice gets more engagement?

Warm beats authoritative. Posts SOMIN tagged as appreciative (80% beat rate, 41 posts), hopeful (79%, 53 posts) and enthusiastic (63%, 145 posts) did much better than authoritative (38%, 297 posts), educational (36%, 74 posts), helpful (29%, 21 posts) or urgent (27%, 22 posts).

The 12-month averages agree: hopeful posts averaged 72.4 and grateful posts 76.3 engagements, against 32.6 for informative, 23.2 for helpful and 21.8 for urgent posts. Emotion tags tell the same story. Posts built on connection (71% beat rate) and gratitude (72%) did well; posts built on frustration (28%) did not. In this category, agitating the buyer's pain earns less than sharing a win.

This fits a wider pattern we describe in why imperfect, authentic posts win: people respond to people, not to polished authority.

Do length, hashtags, questions and timing matter?

Less than the format, the author and the subject. Here is what we found:

  • Length. Page-written posts under 300 characters beat their baseline only 31% of the time. Beyond that, length made little difference (43% to 48%). Long posts did well mainly when a person wrote them.
  • Questions. Page posts that asked the audience a question beat their baseline 40% of the time, against 45% for those that did not. A question at the end does not create a conversation.
  • Hashtags. 77% of posts used none. Posts with one or two hashtags beat their baseline 63% of the time (76 posts), against 47% with three or more and 46% with none. LinkedIn suggests three to five; in this sample, fewer did better, though the groups are small.
  • Day of the week. Weekday beat rates ranged from 44% to 50%. There were too few weekend posts (27) to judge.

What rules can a B2B marketer apply?

Eight rules follow from the data. Test them against your own baseline before you rebuild a calendar around them.

  1. Give your people the pen. Ask employees, executives, customers and partners to write about their work, then reshare the best from the company page.
  2. Default to a single strong image. Save video for when motion carries the message, and treat document carousels as a test, not a habit.
  3. Make about one post in four about people. Hiring, teams, milestones, internships and events are the posts this audience engages with most.
  4. Write warmly. Thanks, pride and optimism outperform urgency and authority. Cut the countdowns.
  5. Rework your how-tos. Educational posts need a named author, a point of view or a real example to compete. A generic tip from a brand page rarely lands.
  6. Write more than a line, but do not pad. Very short page posts underperform; past 300 characters, substance matters more than length.
  7. Use one or two specific hashtags and drop the engagement-bait question.
  8. Benchmark against yourself. Compare each post with your own median, not with a bigger competitor's raw numbers.

What are the limits of this data?

This is one category, 12 vendors and 90 days of public posts. The results are observational, so they show what tends to go with higher engagement, not what causes it. Engagement is not reach, leads or revenue. One vendor, Buffer, supplied a quarter of the posts, which is why we checked the main findings with Buffer removed. Content labels come from SOMIN's models, and LinkedIn reposts are excluded from engagement. Algorithm changes can also shift what works; our post on 2026 algorithm changes covers LinkedIn's recent ones.

FAQ

What type of LinkedIn post gets the most engagement for B2B tech brands?

In our sample of 734 martech vendor posts, company culture posts written by people and shared with a single image did best. Culture posts had a median of 36 engagements and beat their vendor's usual result 70% of the time.

Is video better than images on LinkedIn for B2B?

Not in this category. Single-image posts had a median of 25 engagements against 15 for video, and image posts drew about twice as many comments. The order held for page-written posts on their own.

Should company pages reshare employee posts?

Yes. Posts written by people and reshared by the page earned a median of 31.5 engagements against 15 for page-written posts, and nearly four times the comments. LinkedIn's own Pages guide also recommends resharing employee and executive mentions.

How many hashtags should a B2B LinkedIn post use?

One or two did best in our data, beating the vendor's baseline 63% of the time against 46% with none and 47% with three or more. The groups are small, so test it on your own page.

How do I benchmark LinkedIn engagement fairly?

Compare each post with your own page's median, not with competitors' raw totals, because follower count drives most of the difference. Then look at which formats, topics and tones beat your median most often.
