SOMIN Influencer GMV is the creator-programme solution from SOMIN (SoMin.ai), the AI marketing-intelligence company run by Somin Pte. Ltd. in Singapore. It works out why your top-selling creators sell, gives each creator a territory only they own, and builds a brief in their own voice. The briefs are tuned to grow gross merchandise value (GMV), meaning sales, not likes.

What is SOMIN Influencer GMV?

It is a SOMIN solution, status Live, for brand and creator-marketing teams. The SOMIN Influencer GMV page frames the problem like this: analytics tell you what happened, while this tells you what to change.

It runs on SOMONITOR, SOMIN's real-time category engine. Each creator's content is scored there, so the guidance is grounded in what is selling, not in roster averages. It is built for the team that owns the roster: the Head of Influencer or Social Commerce, the creator manager, and the creators and revenue owners who depend on clear direction.

How does it work, step by step?

SOMIN describes five steps, run for every creator:

  1. Start with your sellers. Take your roster of sales-driving creators on TikTok Shop.
  2. Give each a lane. Each creator gets a distinct territory to own, with no two on the same ground.
  3. Decode the sale. For each creator it works out what drives their sales: what they say, how they present the product and how they frame the hook.
  4. Build the brief. It returns a bespoke brief per creator, saying what to change to lift engagement and sales, with real post examples.
  5. Roll up the roster. It rolls the results into a portfolio view across the roster, plus one deck per creator.

The page says the per-creator brief goes from days to minutes. It also says that once you feed in your creators and competitors, the first territory map runs in minutes.

What is a creator territory?

A territory is the product claim or lane a creator can credibly own, found by comparing the creator with look-alikes and with the category. SOMIN describes the lane as the space the category leaves unclaimed.

The idea matches a wider method. Our guide to marketing whitespace explains how to find unclaimed ground, and the SOMIN Strategy article covers the category-level version. Here it is applied to people on a roster, so the roster stops competing with itself.

What does a creator brief contain?

SOMIN says each brief is presentation-ready and written in the creator's own tone. It names:

  • the territory to own,
  • the message angle,
  • the concept, and
  • the product it is anchored to.

SOMIN also says every concept is pre-scored before spend. Most teams, it says, pass the briefs on as written or use them as a starting point that the creator improves. SOMIN frames the brief as evidence the manager delivers, which sharpens the human conversation and does not replace it. If creators resist AI notes, that framing is the one SOMIN recommends.

What results has SOMIN published?

SOMIN publishes one case: a top-3 global beauty brand's creator-affiliate programme on TikTok Shop. The brand and handles are anonymised under NDA, so the figures cannot be checked independently.

  • SOMIN ranked the roster by real TikTok Shop GMV and narrowed 1,047 creators to 6 real sellers re-briefed.
  • It mapped six territories with zero overlap.
  • The top seller out-earned the runner-up 2.4 times by owning skin-results content.
  • 31% of the top seller's audience sat in an unclaimed makeup lane.
  • The target was to lift that creator's skin-results audience share from 28% to 40%. That is a target, not a result.

The main page also lists "1-in-3 activated creators convert today", and the creators page shows an estimated GMV figure for briefs. Treat estimates as estimates and ask SOMIN how each was measured.
