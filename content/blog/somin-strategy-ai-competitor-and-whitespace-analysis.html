SOMIN Strategy is the strategy agent from SOMIN (SoMin.ai), the AI marketing-intelligence company run by Somin Pte. Ltd. in Singapore. It reads the competitive field and live audience conversation in a category, finds the whitespace no brand owns, and renders a client-ready deck in the client's own brand. SOMIN reports a median of 13 minutes from brief to a stack of pre-scored concepts.

What is SOMIN Strategy?

SOMIN Strategy compresses weeks of senior desk research into a single run. It is built for teams that pitch to win, and it hands the strategic thinking back to the planner.

On its SOMIN Strategy page, SOMIN names its economic buyer as an agency managing director or strategy director, its champion as the new-business lead, and its supporters as account and creative teams. It also works direct to brands, where the buyer is the marketing lead commissioning the work.

How does SOMIN Strategy work?

It follows four steps, as set out on the product page:

  1. Read the field. It reads the competitive field and the live audience conversation in your category through SOMONITOR.
  2. Find the whitespace. It finds the whitespace no one owns, and can rule an angle out, not just confirm it.
  3. Build the picture. It builds the target persona, the cultural tension to lean into, the core idea, and the creative concepts and messaging routes to test.
  4. Render the deck. It outputs a client-ready deck in the client's own brand, either a full briefing or a tighter single-idea pitch for new business.

On the AI agents page, SOMIN adds that the agent decodes more than 4,000 competitor ads per category. It describes this as a figure for that page's example, so check the product page for current numbers.

What is whitespace, and how does SOMIN find it?

Whitespace is a message, audience, need or format that rivals in a category leave unclaimed, where audience demand still exists. SOMIN finds it by parsing every competitor ad for its proposition, persona and tension, then showing what is crowded and what is open.

The idea is explained in full in what marketing whitespace is. In the worked example there, SOMIN analysed 1,740 organic posts from 16 social-listening brands. The centre of the category was crowded, while trust, hopeful tones, testimonials and case studies were left thin. For the same idea across countries, see competitor ad analysis by market.

How is SOMIN Strategy different from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Meltwater and GWI?

They answer different questions. SOMIN's comparison page puts it this way, based on each tool's own public focus:

ToolQuestion it answers
ChatGPTWhat the model and the web can reason and draft
PerplexityWhat the web says, with citations
MeltwaterWhat is being said about brands in media and social
GWIWho the audience is, from representative survey data
SOMIN StrategyWhat is winning in the category's creative now, and what to say next

SOMIN says many teams pair them: define the audience in GWI, watch the conversation in Meltwater and decide the angle in SOMIN. It also says GWI's survey-grade sample is a separate category that SOMIN does not replicate. Competitor tools change, so verify their current features. How is it different from social listening?

Listening tells you what is being said. SOMIN Strategy tells you the strategy behind what brands are saying: each competitor ad is parsed for proposition, persona and tension, and the whitespace left open is mapped. For the wider comparison, see social listening vs audience research vs ad optimisation.

Why does the data matter more than the model?

SOMIN's argument is that the moat is the data, not the model. A general assistant can draft a strategy, but it answers from what the web says in general. SOMIN answers from SOMONITOR, the live conversation in your category, so it reports what is landing today.

The product page frames the alternatives plainly: annual qualitative decks are stale before the campaign window opens, and desk research eats weeks of senior planner time. It also asks why a team would not simply pay about £40 a month for ChatGPT and Perplexity. The answer it gives is that those tools do not decode competitor creative or pre-score a concept. Whether that trade-off suits you depends on the job: exploration and drafting are different from deciding an angle and pitching it.

Is the output really client-ready?

It renders as a deck in the client's brand, but SOMIN is candid about its role. Treat it as a starting brief, not the finished strategy: an accelerant, with senior planners still onside. SOMIN suggests selling it as the research floor that frees planners for the part only they can do.

SOMIN's page cites 78% CTR-prediction accuracy for its SoCraft scoring models, published at ACM WSDM 2023, behind the pre-scored concepts. Read the papers on the research page.

FAQ

How fast is a SOMIN Strategy brief?

SOMIN states a median of 13 minutes from brief to a stack of pre-scored concepts, and points to Google Cloud's customer story on SOMIN as independent documentation.

Does SOMIN Strategy replace my planners?

No. It does the desk research; your planners do the thinking that wins the work, according to SOMIN's own FAQ.

Can it tell me an idea will not work?

SOMIN says it can rule an angle out, not just confirm one, because it answers from the live category conversation instead of general knowledge.

Who is SOMIN Strategy for?

Agency strategy and new-business teams, and brand marketing leads who commission strategy work.
