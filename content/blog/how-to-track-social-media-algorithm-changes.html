To track social media algorithm changes, follow each platform's official explanations, keep a simple log of your own results, and test a suspected change before you react. Most "algorithm update" posts are guesses. A drop in reach can come from your content, your posting pattern, the season or competitors, so check those before you blame the platform.

Key takeaways

  • Use official sources first: platform help pages, transparency pages and statements by platform leads.
  • Keep a weekly log of reach and engagement by format, so you can spot real shifts.
  • Compare with a benchmark, such as competitors in the same category, before you blame the algorithm.
  • Test a change on a small share of posts for a few weeks before you change the whole plan.

Where do platforms explain their ranking?

Each large platform publishes some explanation of how it ranks content. Meta has published system cards that describe how AI systems rank content on Facebook and Instagram, in its Transparency Center. TikTok explains how videos are recommended in its newsroom and help pages. YouTube describes its recommendations on its "How YouTube works" site, and LinkedIn's engineering team writes about its feed. Heads of platforms, such as Instagram's head Adam Mosseri, also share changes in their own posts and videos. These sources rarely give formulas, but they tell you which signals matter.

How do you tell a real change from noise?

  1. Log the basics weekly: posts, reach, engagement and saves or shares, split by format (short video, image, carousel, text).
  2. Look for a pattern across several weeks, not one bad post. A real shift shows up across many posts.
  3. Check other causes: fewer posts, a new format, a holiday, a viral post the week before, or paid support ending.
  4. Compare with peers. If competitors' reach fell at the same time, the cause is likely the platform or the season. If only yours fell, look at your content.

How should you test a suspected change?

Turn the rumour into a question you can test. For example: "Do carousels now reach more people than single images for us?" Post both formats on similar topics for three to four weeks, at similar times, and compare the median result, not the best post. If the difference is small, do not rebuild your plan around it.

What should you change, and what should you keep?

Platforms change details often, but some things hold: content that people watch to the end, save, share or reply to tends to travel further. Keep the parts of your plan that produce those reactions. Change the format mix only when your own data, not a rumour, shows a shift.

Why do competitor benchmarks matter?

Your own account is a small sample. Looking at what competitors post and how their content performs shows whether a format is rising across the category. SOMIN Strategy tracks competitor posts and ads by format and engagement, so you can compare your trend with the category's.

FAQ

How often do social media algorithms change?

Constantly, in small ways. Platforms test and adjust ranking all the time and announce only some changes, which is why your own log matters.

My reach dropped this week. Is it the algorithm?

Not necessarily. Check your posting frequency, formats, timing, the season and competitors' reach first. One week is too short to call a trend.

Where can I find official information?

Platform help centres, transparency pages, engineering blogs and posts by platform leads. Treat third-party claims as hypotheses to test.

Should I change my strategy after every update?

No. Change your format mix when your own data shows a shift over several weeks.
