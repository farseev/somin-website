Why do creative-led pitches lose?

Because they look alike. When every agency answers the same brief with the same category conventions, the client has little to choose on except chemistry and price.

It is also an expensive way to lose. The EACA's 2025 cost-of-pitching study of 412 agencies put the average new-business pitch at €43,804, with five to eight people on each pitch contributing more than 44 hours apiece. The same study found only four in ten winning creative ideas are implemented by the client.

How clients find agencies is shifting as well. RSW/US's 2026 outlook reports that the share of clients finding new firms through networking fell from 73% in 2022 to 58% in 2025, and that clients increasingly want AI-driven insight and analytics rather than vague promises. A pitch that opens with evidence speaks to that buyer.

SOMIN's listening data suggests why sameness happens. In SOMIN's audience-research workspace, an insight built on 65 posts about inefficient advertising strategies (2,061 engagements) reads "To truly connect, dare to diverge from the trending path". Its angle rests on Herd Behaviour Theory: brands follow prevailing trends without checking whether they fit their audience. Pitch decks drift the same way.

What competitor research should a pitch include?

Enough to answer one question convincingly: where can this client win that its competitors are not already standing? Everything in the research should serve that answer.

Senoko Energy: a challenger pitch built on whitespace

For a Senoko Energy pitch, SOMIN read six months of organic content from Senoko and four Singapore rivals, plus a four-creator panel standing in for home owners and families. The category spent an average 46.1% of its voice defending commodity pricing, while creator content for that audience clustered 62.7% on environmental and conservation stewardship. Senoko's own stewardship share had fallen from 20% in March 2026 to zero in its last 90 days.

The Senoko Energy pitch turned that gap into three scored territories, a 39-post, 13-week calendar and a 27-concept creative library, all traceable to 18 SOMIN studies.

What are clients telling agencies they want?

Proof of business outcomes, not activity. SOMIN's ads-optimisation workspace keeps returning to the gap between what agencies report and what advertisers need.

How do you build a data-led pitch in a week?

  1. Fix the competitive set. Four to six brands the client actually loses customers to, plus an audience proxy such as a creator panel or community.
  2. Pull three to six months of content. Organic posts and live ads, so you see what brands say and what they pay to say.
  3. Decode it. Tag every post by persona, proposition, tension and tone, so share of voice is counted rather than guessed.
  4. Score the openings. Compare client, competitor and audience share on each theme, and mark each as crowded, open or a cheap test.
  5. Write the point of view in one sentence. If it needs a paragraph, you have not chosen yet.
  6. Build the creative on the opening. Concepts, a calendar and, where you can, a predicted performance score for each concept.
  7. Keep the trail. Every slide should be able to show the posts behind it when the client asks.

SOMIN's platform for agencies runs steps two to six in one workflow, which is how a category read that once took weeks fits inside a pitch timeline.

What makes competitor evidence persuasive in the room?

Specificity and honesty. A number with its sample size and date beats a confident adjective, and a flagged weakness beats a hidden one.

The Senoko deck is a useful model. It labels its third territory as a low-confidence test, and it reports a prior-year check that pointed the same way while noting it rested on only eight posts. Telling a client what you are less sure of gives them a reason to trust the claims you are sure of.

FAQ

How long should competitor research for a pitch take?

Days, not weeks. KPI Media cut its pitch preparation from one week with three people to two days with one, which left more of the timeline for the idea itself.

What competitor data can agencies use in a pitch?

Publicly available content is the safe base: competitors' organic social posts, ads visible in public ad libraries, websites and public creator content. Analysed together over three to six months, that is enough to map share of voice and whitespace.

How many competitors should a pitch analysis cover?

Usually four to six direct rivals, plus an audience proxy. The Senoko Energy pitch read four Singapore competitors and a four-creator panel over six months.

Do clients care about data in a creative pitch?

They care when the data explains the idea. In SAMY's Nightcap pitch, a competitor-led read moved the conversation from creative services to measurable business impact, and the agency won a multi-million-pound retainer.

Before your next pitch, run the category read before the creative kick-off, not after it. Choose one opening, put its evidence on two slides, and let the creative team build from there. If the research cannot fit the time you have, fix that first: in 2026 the agencies that win are the ones that arrive with the sharpest read of the client's market in the room.
