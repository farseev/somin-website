An AI social media manager agent can run the repeatable parts of social: watching the category, planning a calendar, drafting posts and images, scheduling, and drafting reports. A person should keep brand judgement, public claims, sensitive replies and crisis response. The safest set-up lets the agent prepare everything and a named person approve before anything goes live.

Key takeaways

  • Split social work by task, not by tool: planning, drafting, scheduling, reporting and community replies each carry different risk.
  • Agents suit work that repeats often, is cheap to undo and can be checked against a source.
  • Keep a person on claims, legal and sensitive topics, complaints, and anything that speaks for the brand in a crisis.
  • Choose the autonomy level per task: fully autonomous, gated by sign-off, or draft-only.
  • Use a written checklist at the gate so approval is a check, not a skim.

What is an AI social media manager agent?

It is software that does more than schedule posts. A scheduler publishes what you give it. An agent decides what to post by reading the category, drafts it in your voice and, depending on settings, publishes it.

SOMIN (SoMin.ai), the AI marketing-intelligence company run by Somin Pte. Ltd. in Singapore, offers one as its Social Media Manager. Its page describes a loop of four steps: watch what is landing in your category, write in your voice, compile and publish, and run autonomously or pause for human sign-off. The page says it already runs SOMIN's own LinkedIn channel. See the Social Media Manager page and the wider AI agents suite.

How should you decide what to hand over?

Use three tests: how often the task repeats, whether a mistake can be undone cheaply, and whether the output can be checked against a source. Tasks that pass all three are candidates for automation. Tasks that fail any of them need a person.

This is the same logic we set out in agentic workflows for agencies. Social adds one more factor: a post is public the moment it goes out, so the cost of a mistake is higher than in an internal report.

Planning

Agents are strong here because the work is research-heavy and easy to check. An agent can read what is landing in your category and propose angles. A person decides which fit the brand and the quarter. For the method behind angles, see the persona, tension, angle, story framework.

Drafting

Drafting is where agents save the most time and where mistakes hide best. AI copy reads fluently even when it is wrong, which is why drafting needs a gate. SOMIN's own page describes a sign-off checkpoint where you approve, edit or skip each post, and says autonomy is a setting, not a default you are stuck with.

Scheduling and publishing

Once a post is approved, scheduling is mechanical and low risk. This is the easiest task to automate fully. The one exception is timing: a person should be able to pause the queue when news breaks.

Reporting

Let the agent pull and summarise the numbers. A person reads the draft, checks any figure that will be quoted, and writes the "so what". Every number should trace to a source a reviewer can open quickly.

Community replies

Routine questions, such as opening hours or where to find a product, suit draft replies. Complaints, health or legal questions, and emotional messages do not. Public replies speak for the brand, so use draft-only mode and send them from a named person's account or queue.

What is the human-in-the-loop checklist?

Use the same checklist at every gate. Our QA checklist for AI marketing content covers it in detail. In short, before a post goes live, a person confirms:

  • Facts: every name, date, price and product detail is right.
  • Numbers: every statistic traces to a source that can be opened.
  • Voice: it sounds like the brand and not like every other brand.
  • Claims and legal: no unproven claims, no regulated topics without review.
  • Localisation: wording and references fit the market, not just the language.
  • Context: nothing in the news or in the account's recent replies makes the post look tone-deaf.
  • Accessibility: images have alt text and the text is readable.
  • Owner: a named person signs off, and the decision is recorded.

How do you roll it out safely?

  1. Start gated. Run every task with a sign-off for the first few weeks and record what reviewers change.
  2. Look at the corrections. If the same fix repeats, fix the brief or the voice guide, not the post.
  3. Relax one task at a time. Move scheduling to autonomous first, then low-risk drafts, and leave sensitive replies draft-only.
  4. Keep a pause button. Someone must be able to stop the queue at once.
  5. Review monthly. Check tone, engagement and complaints, and tighten controls if quality slips.

This keeps the agent's speed without handing it the brand's voice. It also protects the people on your team: with the routine work removed, their time goes into judgement, which is the point of rebuilding the junior marketing role.

FAQ

What can an AI social media manager actually do?

It can watch your category, suggest themes, draft posts and images, build a calendar, schedule and publish, and draft reports. How much it does without approval is a setting you choose.

Can an AI agent replace a social media manager?

It can take over repeatable tasks. It does not replace the person who owns the brand voice, handles sensitive replies and decides what the account should stand for. Most teams get the best result by moving that person from producing to reviewing.

Should an AI agent reply to comments and messages?

Draft routine replies, yes. Send complaints, health, legal or emotional messages to a person. Use draft-only mode so a human presses send.

How do I stop an agent posting something off-brand?

Keep a sign-off gate before publishing, give the agent a written voice guide, and review what reviewers change. SOMIN's Social Media Manager lets you approve, edit or skip each post.

Is it worth automating if I need to review every post?

Often yes, because the agent does the research, drafting and scheduling, and review is faster than writing from scratch. Measure time to first draft and the number of corrections. If corrections do not fall, the inputs need work.
