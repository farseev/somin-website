Persona, tension and angle chained into a story per pillar, then propositions written only against capabilities you already own.

Common questions

“We already have AI creative.”

Most brands do. The question is whether anything in the stack tells you which of those creatives to run. The pushbacks we hear most:

How is this different from a generative creative tool?
Those tools produce options. SOMIN decides what the option should say — the territory the category is leaving open, the proposition your brand can actually deliver, and whether the finished frame beats the category baseline. Production is one step of four, and it is the only one most tools cover.
Will it stay on brand?
Production runs in your own Figma file against your real component set, with licensed photography and a composited wordmark — nothing about the identity is generated. Palette, type and copy density are fixed by a locked prompt suffix, so drift is a configuration error rather than a creative risk.
Do we have to give you our Figma file?
No. The Figma production step is optional and gated on an explicit question — it only runs if you hand over a template link. Skip it and you still get the strategy, the propositions and the scored expressions; you just build the final frames yourself.
What does “scored” actually mean?
Each creative gets a predicted clickability class against the category corpus, with a confidence figure. Our gate requires both halves — the class must be above average and confidence must reach 0.80. A high class at low confidence does not qualify, which is the check most pre-flight scoring quietly skips.
How fast does a team adopt it?
The run in the hero started from one chat message. There is no methodology to learn and no data to prepare — name the brand, the moment and the market, and the first whitespace read comes back in minutes.
