A typical organic post from a marketing-technology brand earns about 11 engagements. The average is higher, at 31.5, because a small share of posts takes most of the response. Across 9,079 public posts from 23 vendors in social listening, ad optimisation and audience research, published between 18 October 2025 and 18 October 2026, the mean engagement rate was 0.12% of followers. LinkedIn carried most of the volume, Instagram returned the higher rates, and Facebook barely registered.

How to use this page

The tables below are benchmarks for B2B marketing-technology brands, computed by SOMIN from public organic posts. Start with the vendor category closest to yours, then compare by platform, format, content type and tone. Every table shows the last 12 months and, where the sample allows, the last 90 days. The methodology explains exactly what was counted, and the last section shows how to set your own targets from these numbers. Key numbers

  • Median engagements per post: 11 across all 9,079 posts; 14 for social listening vendors, 11 for audience research firms and 3 for ad optimisation platforms.
  • Average engagements per post: 31.5 over 12 months and 23.9 over the last 90 days.
  • Average engagement rate: 0.12% of followers over 12 months; 0.060% on LinkedIn, 0.30% on Instagram and 0.021% on Facebook.
  • Concentration: 5.7% of posts earned 100 or more engagements, and those posts collected 48% of all engagements. One post in ten earned none.
  • Content and tone: company culture posts averaged 62.6 engagements, twice the overall mean, and hopeful, relatable and excited posts beat informative and authoritative ones. Yet thought leadership appears on 78% of posts and an informative tone on 72%.

How often do martech brands post?

The median vendor in the 12-month set published about six organic posts a week across the tracked channels. By category, the median was about seven a week for social listening vendors, six to seven for audience research firms and four to five for ad optimisation platforms. The range is wide, from a handful of posts a year to about 28 a week, and volume is concentrated: five vendors (Buffer, Emplifi, Meltwater, Agorapulse and Sprinklr) published 67% of the social listening posts.

Methodology

Sources

All figures come from three SOMIN workspaces that track the public organic posts of marketing-technology vendors, aggregated with SOMIN's posts-aggregate tool on 18 October 2026:

  • Social listening and social media management: Meltwater, Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Talkwalker, Statusbrew, Brand24, Agorapulse, Synthesio, Awario, Buffer, Keyhole, Emplifi, Sendible, BuzzSumo, YouScan, Zoho Social and Sprinklr.
  • Ad optimisation: Connectio, Smartly, Adzooma, Madgicx, Strike Social and AdEspresso.
  • Audience research: Milieu, Euromonitor, Nielsen and GWI.

Keyhole and BuzzSumo (and Talkwalker in the 90-day window), Connectio and AdEspresso published nothing on the tracked channels, which is why the vendor counts are lower than the lists. SOMIN's own posts are excluded. So is Digimind Social, which the social listening workspace also tracks: a few of its Instagram posts drew more engagements than the account has followers, which on its own would have more than tripled the category's average engagement rate. With Digimind included, the 12-month social listening figures are 6,391 posts, 35.5 average engagements and a 0.38% average engagement rate.

Windows and counts

  • 12 months: posts published from 18 October 2025 to 18 October 2026; 9,079 posts from 23 vendors.
  • 90 days: posts published from 20 July to 18 October 2026; 1,831 posts from 22 vendors.
  • Channels: LinkedIn company pages, Instagram feed posts (including carousels and Reels), Facebook pages and TikTok. Instagram Stories are excluded because their engagement is not public; 186 Stories were posted in the 12-month window. X and YouTube are not tracked in these workspaces.

Definitions

  • Engagements: likes or reactions, plus comments, plus shares, as SOMIN records them for each post. LinkedIn reposts are tracked separately and are not included.
  • Engagement rate: the engagement rate SOMIN records for each post, which is engagements relative to the publishing account's followers. The average is the mean of those per-post rates, not total engagements divided by total followers.
  • Median: the middle post when all posts are ranked by engagements, found by counting posts in engagement bands.
  • Content type and tone: labels assigned by SOMIN's models from each post's text and visuals. A post can carry several labels.
  • Pooled rows ("all three categories" and the platform, format, content type and tone tables) are weighted by post count.

Limits

  • Averages are pulled up by a few very successful posts; use the median for a single post and the average for a programme of many posts.
  • Engagement rate depends on follower count, so large accounts post low rates even when their raw engagement is high.
  • Volume is concentrated in a few vendors, and a small sample can swing a row, which is why TikTok and tones with fewer than 150 posts are not benchmarked.
  • These are organic posts from B2B software and data vendors. They are not benchmarks for paid ads or consumer brands.
  • Model labels can be wrong on individual posts, so read them as patterns across many posts rather than as a verdict on one.
  • Engagement on the newest posts was still growing when the data was pulled.

How to apply these benchmarks to your own brand

  1. Pick the comparison row: your vendor category, your main platform and the window that matches your reporting period.
  2. Compare medians first. If your median post sits below the category median, fix the everyday post before chasing a viral one.
  3. Compare rates only between accounts of similar size, because engagement rates tend to fall as followings grow.
  4. Check your content mix against the content-type table. If most of your posts are educational or thought leadership, test company culture posts, which earn well, and testimonials and case studies, which few vendors publish.
  5. Do the same for tone: try hopeful, relatable and reflective posts alongside the informative default, and judge them over at least a quarter.
  6. Re-benchmark every quarter against your own direct competitors. SOMIN's social media manager and reporting agent can run this comparison on your own category.

FAQ

What is a good engagement rate for a B2B marketing-technology brand?

In SOMIN's 12-month benchmark of 23 martech vendors, the average engagement rate was 0.12% of followers: 0.060% on LinkedIn company pages, 0.30% on Instagram and 0.021% on Facebook. Beating your platform's figure is a reasonable first target, but compare with accounts of a similar size, because rates tend to fall as followings grow.

How many engagements does a typical martech LinkedIn post get?

LinkedIn company-page posts from the vendors in this benchmark averaged 37.2 engagements over 12 months and 31.5 over the last 90 days. The median post across all channels earned 11, a reminder that most posts land well below the average.

Why do you show both the median and the average?

Because engagement is concentrated. Posts with 100 or more engagements were 5.7% of the sample but collected 48% of all engagements, which pulls the average up. The median tells you what a normal post earns; the average tells you what a whole programme of posts earns per post.

Which content types earn the most engagement in martech?

Company culture posts averaged 62.6 engagements over 12 months, about twice the overall mean, followed by brand offering and community engagement posts at around 34. Educational posts averaged 21.0 and case studies 8.8, although case studies are so rare that their figure rests on only 91 posts.

Why is Digimind Social excluded?

A few of its Instagram posts drew more engagements than the account has followers, which on its own would have more than tripled the social listening category's average engagement rate. The page reports the figures with Digimind included in the methodology so you can see the difference.
