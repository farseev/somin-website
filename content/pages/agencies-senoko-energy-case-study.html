The Trend

The category shouts about price. The audience-directed conversation is somewhere else entirely.

Senoko and its competitors spend an average 46.1% of monthly voice defending commodity pricing — competitor-led at 68.3% — while creator content made for Home Owners & Families clusters overwhelmingly elsewhere: 62.7% on Environmental & Conservation Stewardship alone. Senoko competes hardest where the audience-directed conversation barely exists, and has gone silent exactly where it's sustained.

The retreat: Senoko's only 100%-certified-solar plan, LifeGreen24, already exists — yet Senoko's own share of voice on Environmental & Conservation Stewardship fell from 20% in March 2026 to 0% of its last 90 days, while home & family creators sustained an average 73.8% on the identical theme for six straight months.

  • ✓ Read 6 months of organic content (Feb–Jul 2026) from Senoko and four Singapore rivals — Geneco SG, Tuas Power, SP Group, Flo Energy — plus a 4-creator panel used as an audience proxy for Home Owners & Families.
  • ✓ Senoko's stewardship share of voice: 20% (Mar) → 0% (Jul) → 0% of its last 90 days. A year earlier (Jan–Mar 2025) it held a small n=3/28 toehold in the same theme, since let go to zero.
  • ✓ Home & family creators — Biogirl MJ, Qiyun Woo, Olivia Choong and Melissa Lam — sustained an average 73.8% share on the identical theme for six straight months, peaking at 82.1% in May 2026 and never dropping below 56.7%.
  • ✓ A prior-year check (Jan–Mar 2025, Study 7392) found a directionally supportive 62.5% share on the equivalent theme, though flagged LOW confidence on a small n=8 sample.

The Pillars

Three plays, each built on six months of evidence.

Territory 1 of 3 · Audience Left · Recover

Environmental & Conservation Stewardship

The play — “Green Diary”: a claimable recovery play built on the strongest evidence in the deck — recover the lapsed stewardship conversation before Geneco extends its lead. Open the run with mini-documentary Reels following real Singapore households onto LifeGreen24, and hand the National Day moment to the #TakeCharge movement rather than treating it as a brand-only beat.

Assets: LifeGreen24 (100% certified solar), EcoSmart Homes (SolarShare, Circle of Green, rooftop solar), #TakeCharge · Formats: Mini-documentary Reels, proof-point carousels, National Day pledge content
Territory 2 of 3 · Whitespace · Claim Early

Family & Youth Climate-Digital Education

The play — “Ask Your House”: the cleanest whitespace in the set — open territory, low competitive risk, and it speaks directly to the “Families” half of the brief's audience. Turn the smart meter into a parent-kid first lesson in how the home's power — and its plan — actually works.

Assets: EcoSmart Homes (kid-friendly explainer angle), Smart Rewards family vouchers (Universal Studios Singapore, GrabFood), existing union/CSR children's-education content · Formats: Parent-kid unboxing series, family explainer Reels
Territory 3 of 3 · Whitespace · Low-Confidence Test

Everyday Energy & Home Myths, Decoded

The play — “Energy Myth Tuesday”: an emerging, cheap-to-claim test bet — brand and competitor voice are both at zero here, and it's a natural extension of Senoko's own highest-volume existing content (plan/cost education, 34.5% of Senoko's last 90 days). Bust one hand-me-down energy myth at a time, in 15 seconds flat.

Assets: LifeSavvy24 peak/off-peak myths, LifePower vs. LifeSteady tariff-comparison myths, solar/EV charging cost myths (EcoSmart Homes) · Formats: 15-second myth-bust Reels, plan-literacy carousels

Key Strategic Findings

Three things we'd build the calendar on.

01

The category talks sustainability in headlines; nobody shows families what it feels like at home — and Senoko, the brand that actually powers that home, has gone quietest of all (62.5% → 12.7% of its own category's voice since March, 0% of its last 90 days).

02

Parents are raising eco-conscious, digitally-literate kids with zero help from the brand that actually powers their home. Senoko already has the hands-on assets — EcoSmart Homes, solar — to become that guide, and no one has claimed the role.

03

Families choose electricity plans on hand-me-down myths, not facts. Senoko already has the plan-literacy content to correct them — just not yet in the format families actually stop to read.

Energy for life means showing up for it — in the feed, not just on the bill. To take charge is to take action.

About this case study.

What is a SOMIN agency pitch?
A data-backed pitch deck built from a real read of a brand's category. SOMIN analyzes months of organic content from the brand, its named competitors, and — where useful — a curated creator panel, then turns the patterns into trend analysis, whitespace mapping, content territories, a publishing calendar, and a creative concept library. This example covers 6 months of content from Senoko Energy, four Singapore competitors (Geneco SG, Tuas Power, SP Group, Flo Energy), and a 4-creator home/eco/family panel.
How does SOMIN find marketing whitespace?
It compares how much a brand, its competitors, and real audience-proxy creators publish on a given content theme, to find territory a brand can credibly recover, claim, or test. Here, SOMIN scored three content territories by creator, brand, and competitor share of voice — finding one territory the audience had already left, one clean whitespace, and one cheap test bet.
What creator panel did SOMIN use for this analysis?
A 4-creator panel, profile-curated as Singapore home, eco, and family-living creators, used as a content proxy for the Home Owners & Families audience: Biogirl MJ (Just Keep Thinking), Qiyun Woo (The Weird and Wild), Olivia Choong (The Tender Gardener), and Melissa Lam (Bamboo Straw Girl). The panel is concentrated rather than evenly spread — one creator carries most monthly volume — so its shares are read directionally, not as a precise population estimate.
What's included in the content calendar?
Specific posting slots mapped to real timing triggers drawn from the underlying trend and whitespace data, organized into contiguous, week-aligned territory blocks so every post traces back to evidence. The Senoko calendar ran 39 posts across 13 weeks — 18 on Environmental & Conservation Stewardship, 15 on Family & Youth Climate-Digital Education, and 6 testing Everyday Energy & Home Myths — including a National Day post honestly rescheduled to the nearest real publish day when the holiday fell outside the posting cadence.
How long does this kind of pitch take to build?
This pitch — 18 studies across 6 months of category and creator-panel research, three whitespace-backed content territories, a 13-week/39-post calendar, and a 27-concept creative library — is the kind of work SOMIN's platform typically completes 13x faster than manual research workflows.
