The trend

The category talks price at 46.1%, the audience talks stewardship

Senoko and its rivals spend an average 46.1% of monthly voice on commodity pricing (competitor-led at 68.3%), while creator content for Home Owners & Families centres on Environmental & Conservation Stewardship (62.7%). Senoko competes where the audience conversation barely exists and is silent where it is sustained.

Senoko already sells LifeGreen24, its only 100%-certified-solar plan, yet its stewardship share fell from 20% in March 2026 to 0% of its last 90 days, while home & family creators averaged 73.8% for six months.

The pillars

Three Senoko plays, each built on six months of evidence

Territory 1 of 3 · Audience Left · Recover

Environmental & Conservation Stewardship

The play — “Green Diary”: recover the lapsed stewardship conversation, the deck's strongest evidence, before Geneco extends its lead: mini-documentary Reels of households moving to LifeGreen24, and National Day handed to #TakeCharge, not a brand-only post.

Assets: LifeGreen24 (100% certified solar), EcoSmart Homes (SolarShare, Circle of Green, rooftop solar), #TakeCharge · Formats: Mini-documentary Reels, proof-point carousels, National Day pledge content
Territory 2 of 3 · Whitespace · Claim Early

Family & Youth Climate-Digital Education

The play — “Ask Your House”: the cleanest whitespace, low-risk and aimed at the “Families” half of the audience: the smart meter as a parent-kid lesson in how home power and plans work.

Assets: EcoSmart Homes (kid-friendly explainer angle), Smart Rewards family vouchers (Universal Studios Singapore, GrabFood), union/CSR children's-education content · Formats: Parent-kid unboxing series, family explainer Reels
Territory 3 of 3 · Whitespace · Low-Confidence Test

Everyday Energy & Home Myths, Decoded

The play — “Energy Myth Tuesday”: a cheap test with brand and competitor voice at zero, extending Senoko's highest-volume content (plan/cost education, 34.5% of its last 90 days): one hand-me-down energy myth busted in 15 seconds.

Assets: LifeSavvy24 peak/off-peak myths, LifePower vs. LifeSteady tariff-comparison myths, solar/EV charging cost myths (EcoSmart Homes) · Formats: 15-second myth-bust Reels, plan-literacy carousels
Key findings

Three findings to build Senoko's calendar on

01

The category talks sustainability in headlines but never shows it at home, and Senoko has gone quietest (62.5% → 12.7% of category voice since March, 0% of its last 90 days).

02

Parents raising eco-conscious, digitally literate kids get no help from their energy brand. Senoko has the assets (EcoSmart Homes, solar) to be that guide, and the role is open.

03

Families choose plans on hand-me-down myths; Senoko's plan-literacy content can correct them once it takes a format families stop to read.

FAQ

Senoko Energy case study FAQ

What is a SOMIN agency pitch?
A data-backed pitch from a real category read: months of organic content from the brand, competitors and, if useful, a creator panel, turned into trends, whitespace, territories, a calendar and creative. Here: Senoko, four rivals and a 4-creator panel over 6 months.
How does SOMIN find marketing whitespace?
It compares how much the brand, competitors and audience-proxy creators publish on each theme. For Senoko: one territory to recover, one clean whitespace and one cheap test.
What creator panel did SOMIN use for this analysis?
Four Singapore home, eco and family-living creators as a proxy for Home Owners & Families: Biogirl MJ (Just Keep Thinking), Qiyun Woo (The Weird and Wild), Olivia Choong (The Tender Gardener) and Melissa Lam (Bamboo Straw Girl). One carries most monthly volume, so shares are directional.
What's included in the content calendar?
Slots tied to trend and whitespace triggers: 39 posts over 13 weeks (18 Stewardship, 15 Family & Youth Education, 6 Home Myths), with the National Day post moved to the nearest publish day.
How long does this kind of pitch take to build?
Days rather than weeks: SOMIN typically works 13x faster than manual research. This one: 18 studies, 6 months of data, three territories, 39 posts and 27 concepts.
