How to Grow Faster

Three pillars Aaron can own.

Three tensions map to three pillars, each backed by its own real trend number and SOMIN story insight.

Pillar 1 of 3 · Whitespace · Open, brand-permitted

Influence, engineered.

The gap: Founders are told creator marketing is a series of one-off campaigns; the peers in this room reinforce that. Nobody frames it as an engineered, compounding system.

Why now: “Marketing-as-a-system” content rose 3→32 into an April peak (prior-year unverified); the system framing itself is barely present among peers — 3% share.
Insight: Story #2015 · 2,575 engagement — “Fragments only become a whole through real understanding.”
Pillar 2 of 3 · Emerging · Rising, claimable

Connection that outlasts the event.

The gap: The industry connects intensely at events like Cannes, then goes quiet. There’s no always-on home for the relationships founders actually build.

Why now: Networking/events content emerged in H2, climbing from 0 toward a peak of 6 in May; peers raise it (18% share) but nobody owns the after-the-event frame.
Insight: Story #2014 · 4,071 engagement — “Real connections take time; speed only forges the illusion of one.”
Pillar 3 of 3 · Emerging · Frontier, low competition

The honest creator-AI stack.

The gap: Founders hear that AI will replace creators. The reality — AI for scale, humans for trust — is barely being explained honestly.

Why now: Tech/AI content in the cohort spiked to 16 posts in April; the frame is nascent and mostly unclaimed (4% peer share).
Insight: Story #2013 · 4,684 engagement — “AI scales the work; the human still wins the trust.”

Questions

About this case study.

What is a SOMIN individual creator strategy?
A SOMIN individual creator strategy reads twelve months or more of a named creator’s real content alongside a defined peer set, then turns the pattern into a whitespace map, content pillars, and a publishing calendar. This example reads Vamp CEO Aaron Brooks against five peer founders — Ben Jeffries, Adam Rivietz, Mae Karwowski, Ryan Detert and Mason Dettloff — across 12 months of Entrepreneur-audience content.
How does SOMIN find a creator’s whitespace?
SOMIN scores content strands by how much a creator and their peers publish on each theme. A strand counts as whitespace when peers barely cover it and supply is rising; if a creator is silent everywhere, that silence alone isn’t whitespace — the strand also needs real momentum and permission for the creator to own it. Aaron Brooks sits at 0% share on every strand, but only “creator marketing as one system” (3% peer share, rising) scored as true whitespace.
What does SOMIN recommend when a creator has near-zero share of voice?
Not a generic “post more” plan — three specific moves in order: lead with the whitespace thesis no peer is saying, claim the emerging territory before a peer names it, and bring an honest, differentiated point of view to the frontier topic. For Aaron Brooks, that’s leading with creator marketing as an engineered system, owning the after-the-event relationship space, and giving an honest read on AI in creator marketing.
Is this based on real engagement data?
Yes. SOMIN reads twelve months of real published content (Aug 2025–Jul 2026) across Aaron Brooks and five peer founders, plus the actual engagement figures on the stories used to build the strategy. It is an engagement-only run — a content-supply and audience-response signal, not a report of sales, revenue, or a measured audience panel.
